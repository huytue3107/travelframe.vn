# TravelFrame Field Notes

Brand identity system for the TravelFrame VJ series.

## 1. Status and scope

This document is approved for the `TravelFrame Field Notes` content series.

It defines the series identity for Reel covers, video titles, lower thirds,
episode markers, social posts and production templates. It does not replace the
overall TravelFrame brand identity or resolve the open TravelFrame brand
architecture.

The system starts with one primary VJ but must remain usable when more VJs join.
The series belongs to TravelFrame; it is not an independent sub-brand.

## 2. Reference direction

The approved direction is **Pop Field Notes**.

The user-provided reference image established these useful visual traits:

- Large, immediate type over real travel footage.
- Pastel paper-like color blocks.
- A mix of bold sans serif and editorial serif type.
- Slightly imperfect placement that feels assembled by hand.
- High contrast and a clear mobile-first reading order.
- Playful energy without decorative complexity.

TravelFrame should use these principles as inspiration, not reproduce the
reference composition, wording or exact styling.

## 3. Brand core

### Name

`TravelFrame Field Notes`

### Brand architecture

```text
TravelFrame
└── TravelFrame Field Notes
    ├── Episode
    ├── Topic or place
    └── VJ identification
```

### Role of the VJ

The VJ is a curious local friend who goes first, pays attention and reports
what the experience actually feels like. The VJ is a human guide to the story,
not the owner of the series identity.

### Audience

International travelers looking for useful, specific and less obvious ways to
experience Vietnam.

### Brand promise

> We go first, so you know what the trip really feels like.

### Personality

- Curious
- Playful
- Candid
- Useful
- Locally aware

### The series is not

- Formal travel news.
- Distant luxury editorial.
- A shock-content channel.
- A generic influencer template.
- Proof that a place, route, price or service is currently available.

## 4. Verbal identity

### Voice

Write like a warm local friend speaking to one traveler:

- Use short, concrete sentences.
- Lead with an observation, question or useful tension.
- Prefer first-hand language when the VJ genuinely visited or tested something.
- Separate opinion from verified fact.
- Avoid exaggerated tourism copy.

### Hook patterns

Approved structural patterns:

```text
Would you stay here?
We got off anyway.
Here is what arrived.
Worth the trip?
The road ends here. The story does not.
What the map leaves out.
```

These are pattern examples, not standing factual claims.

### Episode naming

Preferred structure:

```text
TravelFrame Field Notes / Part 01
[Primary topic or place]
[Question, reaction or useful tension]
```

Examples:

```text
TravelFrame Field Notes / Part 01
A homestay past the road
Would you stay here?
```

```text
TravelFrame Field Notes / Part 02
The train stop tourists miss
We got off anyway.
```

Avoid unsupported superlatives such as `most remote`, `best`, `secret` or
`untouched` unless evidence has been reviewed for the specific publication.

## 5. Color system

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Field Ink | `#171317` | Primary text, stamps and strong contrast |
| Paper White | `#FFFDF7` | Warm neutral background |
| Signal Pink | `#FF9ED2` | Primary hook strip |
| Soft Blush | `#F5D9EB` | Episode marker and quieter accent |
| Map Lemon | `#FFF7B2` | Main topic or location strip |
| TravelFrame Teal | `#008AA3` | Parent-brand endorsement only |

### Color rules

- Use Field Ink with Paper White for the base system.
- Use Signal Pink for the most conversational or emotional line.
- Use Map Lemon for the factual topic, place or category.
- Use Soft Blush for episode numbers and editorial details.
- Use TravelFrame Teal sparingly in the TravelFrame endorsement, a small rule
  or a compact verification device.
- A single frame should normally contain no more than pink, lemon and one
  parent-brand accent.
- Do not use gradients, glow or multicolor backgrounds.

`#008AA3` is a bridge to the current canonical TravelFrame visual source. If the
parent identity changes, update only the endorsement token after review; do not
redesign the complete series system by default.

## 6. Typography

### Primary display

- Preferred: `Archivo Black`
- Fallback: `Arial Black`, `Arial`, sans-serif
- Use: hooks, place names and primary cover titles
- Case: uppercase for compact titles; sentence case for conversational hooks
- Weight: 800-900
- Line height: 0.90-1.00
- Letter spacing: 0

### Editorial accent

- Preferred: `DM Serif Display`
- Fallback: `Georgia`, serif
- Use: episode numbers, `Part 01`, short human notes
- Weight: regular
- Line height: 0.95-1.10
- Letter spacing: 0

### Utility and captions

- Preferred: `DM Sans`
- Fallback: `Arial`, sans-serif
- Use: VJ name, location, subtitles, metadata and CTA
- Weight: 500-700
- Line height: 1.20-1.45
- Letter spacing: 0

### Type rules

- Use no more than two type families in one frame.
- Keep the primary hook to one or two short lines when possible.
- Never condense, stretch or distort type.
- Break lines by meaning, not merely to fill a rectangle.
- Keep all important text inside platform-safe zones.
- Never cover the VJ's eyes or the action needed to understand the shot.

## 7. Core graphic devices

### Paper strip

A solid rectangular color field behind text.

- Corners remain square.
- A rotation of approximately one to three degrees is allowed.
- Text has comfortable horizontal padding.
- The strip may extend beyond the text but should not span the frame by default.

### Episode badge

An editorial serif label such as `Part 04` or `Field Note 04`.

- Default color: Soft Blush.
- Optional hard offset shadow in Field Ink.
- Do not turn it into a rounded pill.

### Field stamp

A compact outlined device for production states that are true and documented.

Examples:

```text
CHECKED ON LOCATION
VJ FIELD NOTE
TRAVELFRAME TEST
```

Do not use `VERIFIED`, `APPROVED` or similar language unless the relevant fact
and approval have actually been recorded.

### Spark

A simple four-point sparkle may be used as a minor separator or motion cue.

- Maximum two visible sparkles in one composition.
- Use white, Field Ink or TravelFrame Teal.
- Do not build decorative patterns from repeated sparkles.

## 8. Series lockup

Preferred text lockup:

```text
[teal square] TRAVELFRAME FIELD NOTES
```

Rules:

- Place in the upper-left safe zone by default.
- Keep it secondary to the episode hook.
- Use DM Sans or the approved utility sans serif.
- The teal square is the parent-brand endorsement device.
- Do not create a separate symbol that competes with the TravelFrame name.
- Do not attach a permanent VJ name to the master lockup.

## 9. Reel cover system

### Reading order

1. Episode badge.
2. Topic, place or situation.
3. Conversational hook.
4. VJ identification.
5. TravelFrame Field Notes endorsement.

### Standard composition

```text
TOP SAFE ZONE
TravelFrame Field Notes endorsement

UPPER-MIDDLE
Part 01
Topic or place
Question or reaction

LOWER SAFE ZONE
With [VJ name] / Local VJ
```

### Cover rules

- Use one dominant hook and no more than three text strips.
- Keep the location or VJ action visible.
- Use real episode footage whenever available.
- Use the strongest readable frame, not necessarily the prettiest landscape.
- Keep text large enough to read at feed-thumbnail size.
- Do not use blur overlays to compensate for a weak frame.
- Do not use an unrelated stock image as evidence of a visited location.

## 10. VJ identity

### On-screen label

Preferred:

```text
WITH [NAME] / LOCAL VJ
```

Alternatives:

```text
[NAME] / TRAVELFRAME VJ
FIELD NOTE WITH [NAME]
```

### Presentation

- The VJ should appear in action, observing or interacting with the location.
- Direct-to-camera delivery should feel conversational, not like a news desk.
- Wardrobe may vary by episode; do not require a fixed costume.
- Favor a recognizable repeated detail such as a field notebook, microphone
  treatment, camera strap or compact teal accessory.
- Do not make the identity dependent on one person's hairstyle, clothing or
  physical features.

### Adding another VJ

Keep the master palette, type, episode badge and lockup unchanged. Only replace:

- VJ name.
- Portrait or footage.
- Optional personal micro-accent approved for that VJ.

## 11. Motion identity

### Entry motion

- Paper strips enter with a quick lateral slide or short cut-on action.
- The episode badge may land with a subtle two-frame offset.
- Sparkles may appear once as a brief punctuation mark.
- Text should settle quickly enough for mobile reading.

### Transitions

Preferred:

- Hard cut.
- Whip pan matched to camera movement.
- Paper-strip wipe.
- Match cut between VJ action and destination detail.

Avoid:

- Long logo animations.
- Liquid morphing.
- Neon glow.
- Excessive bounce.
- Transition packs that overpower real footage.

### End frame

```text
TRAVELFRAME FIELD NOTES
What should we check next?
@travelframe.vn
```

The question may change by episode. Do not add a booking or offer CTA unless the
content objective and downstream flow have been approved.

## 12. Photography and footage

- Prioritize real location texture, VJ action and human-scale details.
- Preserve natural light and recognizable environmental color.
- Use wide context, medium VJ action and close detail within each episode.
- Keep some real ambient sound when consent and production conditions allow.
- Avoid generic drone montages as the only proof of place.
- Do not stage a claim as a real discovery without clear labeling.
- Confirm rights and consent before using identifiable people or private spaces.

AI-generated images may support concept development but must not be presented as
evidence of a real visit, property, person or available experience.

## 13. Accessibility and legibility

- Maintain strong contrast between every text strip and its lettering.
- Do not place white text directly over a bright or complex area.
- Subtitle spoken content.
- Keep captions concise and synchronized with speech.
- Test cover readability at small mobile thumbnail size.
- Avoid rapid flashing graphic devices.
- Important meaning must not depend on color alone.

## 14. Always and never

### Always

- Show one clear story per frame.
- Keep the real place or VJ action visible.
- Use the TravelFrame endorsement consistently.
- Make text placement feel human but intentional.
- Separate first-hand observation, opinion and verified fact.
- Re-check dated travel facts before publication.

### Never

- Cover the VJ's face with the main title.
- Use more than three competing text strips.
- Add rounded pills, gradients, glow or heavy soft shadows.
- Present an unsupported superlative as fact.
- Use testimonial, access, price, availability or safety claims without proof.
- Treat a render or approved design as published content.

## 15. Production checklist

Before design:

- [ ] Episode has a stable working title.
- [ ] Hook is short enough for the cover.
- [ ] Footage and people have appropriate usage rights and consent.
- [ ] Location and travel claims have been checked for the publication date.

Before export:

- [ ] TravelFrame Field Notes lockup is present.
- [ ] Episode badge, title and hook follow the reading order.
- [ ] VJ face and essential action remain visible.
- [ ] Text is readable at mobile thumbnail size.
- [ ] No unsupported claim appears in the visual.
- [ ] Subtitles and safe zones have been checked.

Before publish:

- [ ] Final asset has content and brand review.
- [ ] Caption and CTA match the approved objective.
- [ ] Platform preview has been checked.
- [ ] Publication approval and platform evidence are recorded separately.

## 16. Governance

Brand Strategy owns this document. Creative Production applies the system.
Marketing Growth owns episode messaging and distribution. Publication remains a
separate approval step.

Changes that require Brand Strategy review:

- Master palette or typography.
- Series name or promise.
- Master lockup.
- Permanent graphic devices.
- A new VJ-specific identity layer.

Changes that may vary by episode:

- Topic and hook copy.
- Location imagery.
- Episode number.
- VJ name.
- One-off production labels that are accurate.
