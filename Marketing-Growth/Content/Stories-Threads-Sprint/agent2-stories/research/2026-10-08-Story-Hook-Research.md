## 20 original Story hook examples

These are reusable openings, written for adaptation rather than copy/paste. Every factual or time-sensitive payoff still needs a fresh source check before posting.

### Practical travel utility

1. “First day in Hanoi? This tiny detail saves a lot of guesswork.” → reveal one verified practical detail → manual poll: “Want more like this?”
2. “The number on this Vietnamese note is easier to misread than you think.” → show the denominations clearly → manual quiz; currency fact must be verified against a current authoritative source.
3. “Hungry in Hanoi but unsure what to say?” → one short, checked ordering phrase → manual quiz: choose the correct phrase.
4. “Packing for Hanoi? Leave room for this one useful thing.” → reveal one item with a clear reason → manual poll: “Already packed / Not yet.”
5. “One small phrase makes ordering bánh mì feel less intimidating.” → teach the phrase and pronunciation → manual question box for the next phrase.

### Food and small discoveries

6. “The first bite is not the only thing to notice.” → reveal texture, sound, or serving detail visible in TravelFrame-owned footage → manual slider.
7. “Would you choose the classic filling or try something new?” → show only verified menu options → manual poll.
8. “A Hanoi food stop can start with one simple question.” → give a safe, verified ordering prompt → manual quiz.
9. “This is the part of bánh mì people notice before the filling.” → reveal the bread texture/technique shown in the source → manual poll.
10. “Can you guess what makes this cup different?” → reveal the specific drink only when the image/source supports it → manual quiz.

### Hanoi streets and local context

11. “This Hanoi scene changes when you look one step higher.” → reveal a sign, balcony, or façade detail actually visible → manual poll.
12. “The street is telling two stories at once.” → pair a wide scene with one grounded detail → manual question box.
13. “A quiet corner can be the best part of a busy city.” → show a real TravelFrame Hanoi image, no invented location claim → manual slider.
14. “What would you notice first on this walk?” → show a verified scene → manual poll: “People / Details.”
15. “The useful Hanoi tip is sometimes just knowing when to pause.” → give a safe, non-time-sensitive observation → manual question box.

### Photo confidence and participation

16. “Freeze when the camera comes out? Try one small move.” → demonstrate a slow walk into frame → manual poll: “Walking / Standing.”
17. “A photo can feel more like your trip when the street stays in frame.” → show the composition change → manual poll: “Close-up / Wider scene.”
18. “No pose list? Start with one detail you actually like.” → show an easy look/gesture prompt → manual slider.
19. “Which frame feels more like a real travel memory?” → show two TravelFrame-owned images → manual poll.
20. “What do you want to remember about Hanoi besides the landmark?” → show a genuine street moment → manual question box; invite replies without promising an unapproved guide or offer.

## Suggested sequence rhythm

For one daily chain, use 4–6 frames: (1) a one-line curiosity gap, (2) a concrete reveal, (3) a useful action or second reveal, (4) one native sticker interaction, then (5) a soft DM prompt when it fits. Keep each frame to one idea, retain a consistent type treatment, and make the sequence understandable with sound off. Put the interactive frame in `MANUAL` mode; mark photo-only frames `API`. The Meta runbook describes existing Meta tools and publishing checks, while the orchestration brief states that Story API publishing does not provide poll, quiz, question, or link stickers.
