# Vietnam Photoshoot Ideas: 6 Styles to Shoot in Hanoi

When people plan a Vietnam photoshoot, they usually mean a few hours with a photographer somewhere that looks like Vietnam. Hanoi makes that easy. A lake, a cathedral, lantern streets, French-era facades and a red incense village are all within reach in one morning.

This guide covers six styles we shoot in Hanoi, where each one works, what to wear, and what it costs.

## Quick answer

- **Where to base it:** Hanoi's Old Quarter and Hoàn Kiếm Lake. Many spots are a short walk apart.
- **When to shoot:** early morning or late afternoon for soft light. After dark for lanterns and lit cafés.
- **How long:** one hour covers one area. Two hours covers two or three.
- **What it costs with us:** from 30 USD per hour. Details below.
- **What to skip:** posing on the Train Street rails. It is a residential railway, and we don't shoot there.

## The six styles at a glance

| Style | Good for | Where in Hanoi | What to wear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Couple and honeymoon | Honeymoons, anniversaries, pre-wedding | Lake paths, the cathedral, lamp-lit cafés | Matching tones, not matching outfits |
| Solo traveller | Anyone travelling alone | Old Quarter lanes, Long Biên Bridge walkway | One bright piece that stands out on busy streets |
| Áo dài | A keepsake in traditional dress | Hoàn Kiếm Lake, lantern streets | An áo dài in a colour your backdrop doesn't have |
| Friends and family | Groups, kids, grandparents | Lakeside benches, café windows | Two or three coordinated colours |
| Incense village | A half-day trip out of the city | Quảng Phú Cầu, about 35 km south | White, black or soft green against the red |
| Fashion and street style | Lookbooks, profile refreshes | Đồng Xuân Market, shutters, cyclos | Two looks, bold colours, comfortable shoes for between spots |

## 1. Couple and honeymoon

Couples often come home from Hanoi with hundreds of photos and very few of both of them. A walk fixes that. You stroll, talk and hold hands, and the photographer tells you the small things, like where to look.

Daytime works by the lake and in quiet lanes. After dark, the cathedral and the café fronts glow gold. For a pre-wedding look, bring a suit or gown and shoot against French-era facades.

## 2. Solo traveller

Travelling alone means a camera roll full of landmarks. A solo walk gets you the photos a selfie can't: the full outfit from across the street, you small in front of the cathedral, the laugh you didn't plan.

Pro move: keep moving between shots. The natural ones come right after the posed ones.

Read more: [Solo travel in Hanoi, natural photos without awkward posing](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/solo-female-travel-hanoi-how-get-natural-photos-without-awkward-posing), and see [Hanna's street style session on Long Biên Bridge](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hanoi-street-style-photoshoot-harry-hanna).

## 3. Áo dài

The áo dài is Vietnam's long, fitted tunic, worn over loose trousers. It moves when you walk, which is why it photographs so well.

Pick a colour your backdrop doesn't have. Red and gold stand out in the Old Quarter. Deep blue and white look calm by the lake. Walk slowly and let the front panel move.

## 4. Friends and family

On group trips, someone is always missing from the photos, usually the person holding the phone. With a photographer along, everyone is in every shot.

Start with the whole group while energy is high, then pairs, then a few portraits. Let the slowest walker set the pace, and plan a café stop halfway.

Read more: [Family and group photoshoot in Hanoi](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/family-photoshoot-hanoi). [VERIFY]

## 5. Incense village day trip

In Quảng Phú Cầu, a craft village in Ứng Hòa district some 35 km south of Hanoi, families make incense by hand. On dry days they fan out bundles of red sticks to dry, and whole yards turn red.

It is a working village, so ask before you step into a yard and walk around the bundles. Rain keeps the bundles inside, so keep a backup day.

Read more: [Quảng Phú Cầu incense village photo guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/cam-phu-cau-incense-village-photography-guide).

## 6. Fashion and street style

Hanoi's streets make a free studio. A closed metal shutter, a market sign, a parked motorbike or a cyclo can all be a backdrop.

Always ask the owner before you sit on a bike or lean on a shopfront. Bring two looks, and one walk becomes two shoots.

See it in practice: [Thin at Đồng Xuân Market](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hanoi-old-quarter-photoshoot-harry-thin) and [Wuchr's night session](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hanoi-night-photoshoot-harry-wuchr).

## Where not to shoot: Train Street

Photos on the rails of Hanoi's Train Street are all over social media. It is a residential railway corridor, and access changes with local rules. We don't include it in any route, and we don't pose guests on the tracks.

If you visit, check the current rules on the day, respect the people who live there, and never wait on the tracks. Our [Train Street safety guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hanoi-train-street-2026-safety-first-guide-and-better-railway-photo-spots) has the details.

## FAQ

**Do I need to know how to pose?**
No. Most of a walk is walking and talking. The photographer gives you small actions, like walking toward the camera or looking back over your shoulder.

**Is one hour enough?**
For one area, yes. For two or three areas, or day into night, book two hours.

**Can I wear an áo dài?**
Yes. On VIP an outfit is included, and it can be an áo dài. [VERIFY] On Basic and Premium, bring your own.

**What if it rains?**
