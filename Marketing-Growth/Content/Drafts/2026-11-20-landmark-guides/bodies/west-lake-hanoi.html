West Lake (Hồ Tây) is Hanoi's largest lake: about 500 hectares of open water a few kilometres north of the Old Quarter. Not to be confused with the West Lake in Hangzhou, China, Hanoi's version is where the city goes to breathe. There are sunsets over open water, a long shoreline road for cycling, old temples on the banks, and cafés and restaurants along Xuân Diệu and Thụy Khuê streets.

Most visitors come for one sunset or one bike ride. If you have half a day, you can do both and add Trấn Quốc Pagoda on the way.

Quick answer

  • Where: Tây Hồ and Ba Đình districts, about 3 km north of Hoàn Kiếm Lake; 10–15 minutes by Grab from the Old Quarter outside rush hour.
  • Size: about 500 hectares, with a shoreline of roughly 15–17 km depending on the route you follow.
  • Cost: free. The main temples are free too.
  • Best time: late afternoon into sunset, from Thanh Niên Road or the shore near Trấn Quốc Pagoda.
  • How long: 1–2 hours for sunset and a short walk; about 1.5 hours to cycle the full loop.

What to do at West Lake

Watch the sunset

The classic spot is Thanh Niên Road, the strip between West Lake and the smaller Trúc Bạch Lake. Stand near the causeway to Trấn Quốc Pagoda and the sun drops behind the pagoda across the water. Arrive 30–40 minutes before sunset to find a good spot.

Cycle the loop

A path follows most of the shoreline. Cycling the full loop takes about 1.5 hours at an easy pace; walking it takes 3–4 hours. You can rent bikes from shops around the lake, or start from the Old Quarter. Electric buggies also run from Trấn Quốc Pagoda past the main temples if you would rather sit.

Temples on the shore

  • Trấn Quốc Pagoda: Hanoi's oldest pagoda, on an islet off Thanh Niên Road. Closed over lunch; see our opening hours and dress code guide.
  • Quán Thánh Temple: at the southern corner by Trúc Bạch Lake. Read our Quán Thánh Temple guide.
  • Phủ Tây Hồ (Tây Hồ Temple): on the northern shore, with altars to Vietnam's mother goddesses. Free, usually open 6:00–19:00 and later on the 1st and 15th of the lunar month, when it gets very busy.

Trúc Bạch Lake

Thanh Niên Road separates West Lake from Trúc Bạch, a smaller, calmer lake with swan pedal boats and lakeside restaurants. It is a good place to sit after sunset.

Quảng Bá flower market

On the eastern side, on the Nghi Tàm dyke, Quảng Bá is a wholesale flower market that runs through the night, roughly from 20:00 to 6:00. It is busiest between 2:00 and 5:00, when traders load bikes and trucks. It is a rare night-time scene, but go by taxi and expect to stay up late or start very early. For a gentler version, our sunrise photo walk combines flower markets with Long Biên Bridge.

Eat and drink

The eastern Xuân Diệu strip is lined with cafés, restaurants and boutique hotels. The southern shore along Thụy Khuê has seafood restaurants.

Photo tips

  • Sunset: expose for the sky and let the pagoda go dark; the silhouette reads better than a flat, bright shot.
  • Portraits: on Thanh Niên Road, put the lake behind your subject and shoot with the sun low behind them for a warm rim of light.
  • Blue hour: stay 15–20 minutes after sunset, when the city lights come on across the water.
  • Weather: hazy days soften the sunset colours. Clear days after rain give the strongest skies.

FAQ

Is West Lake Hanoi worth visiting?

Yes, especially for a sunset and a break from the Old Quarter. It is open, quieter and has some of the city's best evening light.

How far is West Lake from the Old Quarter?

About 3 km north. A Grab car takes 10–15 minutes outside rush hour.

How long does it take to cycle around West Lake?

About 1.5 hours at an easy pace for the full loop. Walking it takes 3–4 hours.

Where is the best place to watch the sunset?

Thanh Niên Road, near the causeway to Trấn Quốc Pagoda, where the sun sets behind the pagoda across the lake.

Is it the same as West Lake in Hangzhou?

No. Hangzhou's West Lake is in China. Hanoi's West Lake, Hồ Tây, is in the north of Vietnam's capital.
