St. Joseph's Cathedral Hanoi: Visiting Guide and Christmas Eve Tips

Soot-blackened stone, twin bell towers and stained glass shipped from France in 1886. When to visit, where to shoot it from and what Christmas Eve at Hanoi's cathedral is really like.


The first time the cathedral actually stopped me, rather than just registering as a landmark I already knew, was on a grey afternoon in the rain, around 4pm, when the stone had gone almost black with the wet and the two bell towers looked like they were cut out of the sky rather than standing under it. That soot-darkened, weathered look isn't neglect — it's just what almost a century and a half of Hanoi weather does to stone, and it's exactly what makes the building feel so much like Notre-Dame de Paris, which was clearly the intention from the start.

Construction began in 1884 and the cathedral was finished and consecrated in December 1886, which makes it one of the very first major structures the French colonial administration put up in Hanoi — they were making a statement, and they built it to last. It went up on the site of Bao Thien Pagoda, an eleventh-century Ly dynasty structure that had collapsed centuries earlier, which is a detail I think about every time I'm standing on Nha Chung Street: this ground has been sacred, in one religion or another, for close to a thousand years. The stained glass was made in France and shipped over, the twin bell towers rise about 103 feet with five bells between them, and the interior has that rib-vaulted ceiling you'd expect, paired unexpectedly with a gilded, polished-wood sanctuary that leans into Vietnamese decorative style rather than copying Europe outright — that mix is the most interesting thing about the inside, honestly, more than the height of the ceiling.

It wasn't always open. Under communist rule the cathedral was effectively closed for years, and Mass only resumed on Christmas Eve of 1990 — a detail that changes how you read the crowds you sometimes see there now, packed for evening Mass or lined up on Christmas Eve. It's still the seat of the Archdiocese of Hanoi and a fully working church, not a museum, so weekday afternoons are quiet but Sunday mornings are not.

Best light, and where to sit for it

Locals will tell you the cathedral photographs best between 3:00 and 5:00pm, when the sun angles low enough to rake across the facade and pick out the texture in that blackened stone instead of flattening it. If you don't want to shoot from street level with tourists and cyclos in frame, get a table on the upper balcony of one of the cafes directly across the small square — Cong Ca Phe is the well-known one, with its retro wartime decor and coconut coffee, but there are a few others with similar elevated views. Order slowly, let the light shift, and you'll get a much cleaner shot than fighting the crowd at the gate.

Dress code matters here more than at most Hanoi sites — shoulders and knees covered if you want to go inside, and keep voices down during active services. Outside of Mass times, the doors are usually open for a quieter look at the interior.

This corner of the Old Quarter — cathedral, cafe balcony, that particular afternoon light — is one of the more requested stops on our photo walks, and it's easy to see why once you've sat across from it with a coffee for twenty minutes.

St. Joseph's Cathedral at Christmas

December is the cathedral's biggest month. A giant Christmas tree and a nativity scene go up in the square, and the twin towers are lit in color at night. Many Hanoians who are not Catholic come just for the atmosphere and the photos.

The cathedral on Christmas Eve 2017. Photo: Peter Addor, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

What Christmas Eve is like

  • Masses: Hanoi churches hold Christmas Eve vigil Masses from about 6pm until midnight. The main celebration is usually around 9–10pm.
  • Crowds: By 9pm the square is packed, and from about 11pm the surrounding streets are almost full. At midnight the bells ring in Christmas Day.
  • Traffic: In past years, police closed the street in front of the cathedral to vehicles from late afternoon on December 24. Arrive on foot.
  • Weather: Expect around 15°C at night, sometimes with drizzle. See Hanoi weather in December.

In 2026, Christmas Eve falls on a Thursday. The parish posts its exact Mass times in December. We will add them here once they are published.

Where to stand (and when)

  • For photos: come between 3:30 and 5pm, before the evening crowd. The low sun lights the facade and the tree.
  • For the atmosphere: arrive by 7pm and claim a spot near the edge of the square, close to an exit street.
  • For a café view: the balconies across the square fill up early. Get there before 6pm.
  • For Mass: enter at least an hour early, dress modestly and keep phones on silent.
  • For a calmer visit: go on the morning of December 25, which is a normal working day in Vietnam.

Quieter churches nearby

Cửa Bắc Church (56 Phan Đình Phùng) and Hàm Long Church (21 Hàm Long, completed in 1934) both decorate for Christmas with smaller crowds. Cửa Bắc has listed a Sunday Mass in English in recent years. Check the notice board for current times.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an entrance fee for St. Joseph's Cathedral?

No. It is a working church, not a museum. Visit outside Mass times and dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered.

What time is Christmas Eve Mass at St. Joseph's Cathedral?

Evening Masses on December 24 usually run from about 6pm, with the main celebration around 9–10pm and the bells at midnight. Exact times for 2026 are posted by the parish in December.

Can non-Catholics attend Mass?

Yes. Visitors are welcome to attend quietly. Don't use flash, and don't walk around during the service.

What is the best time to photograph the cathedral?

Between about 3 and 5pm, when the low sun rakes across the dark stone. In December, the Christmas lights come on around sunset, about 5:20pm.
