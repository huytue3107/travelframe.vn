Is Christmas Celebrated in Vietnam? Traditions, Food and How to Say "Merry Christmas"

Christmas isn't a public holiday in Vietnam, yet millions go out on Christmas Eve. How Catholic families and everyone else celebrate, what people eat and the Vietnamese words you'll hear.

Yes, Christmas is celebrated in Vietnam, just not in the way most visitors expect.

It is not a public holiday. December 25 is a normal working day. But on the night of December 24, the big cities fill with people heading to churches, decorated streets and malls. For Vietnam's Catholics, it is one of the most important nights of the year. For almost everyone else, it is a festive night out.

The short answer

  • Public holiday? No. Vietnam's year-end holiday is New Year's Day, January 1. The biggest holiday of the year is Tết, the Lunar New Year, which falls on February 6 in 2027.
  • Who celebrates? Around 7 million Catholics, roughly 7% of the population, celebrate it as a religious feast. Many non-Catholics join in the street celebrations.
  • When? The main celebration is the evening of December 24, not Christmas Day.
  • How? Mass, nativity scenes and family meals for Catholics. Lights, photos, gifts for children and dinner with friends for everyone else.

How Christmas came to Vietnam

Catholic missionaries reached Vietnam in the 16th and 17th centuries. Some of them helped develop the Latin-based alphabet Vietnamese uses today. During the French colonial period, large churches went up across the country, including St. Joseph's Cathedral in Hanoi, completed for Christmas 1886.

Vietnam now has the fifth-largest Catholic population in Asia. Some areas are strongly Catholic. In southern Đồng Nai province, Catholics make up nearly 30% of the population, and in the north, the stone cathedral at Phát Diệm in Ninh Bình has been a pilgrimage site since the 1890s.

How Catholic families celebrate

  • Christmas Eve Mass: churches hold vigil Masses from early evening until midnight. Parishes in Catholic villages fill their streets with lights and processions.
  • The nativity grotto (hang đá): every parish builds one, often huge and creative, with lights, rocks and moving figures.
  • Family dinner: after Mass, families eat together. In the north this looks a lot like a Tết meal, with boiled chicken, sticky rice and spring rolls.

How everyone else celebrates

For most young Vietnamese, Christmas Eve is a social night. Friends and couples ride into the city center to see the lights, take photos in front of trees and churches and eat out. Shops and cafés compete with decorations from late November.

Children get small gifts, and hiring a "Santa" to deliver presents to the house has become popular in big cities. Some offices hold year-end parties around the same time.

The result can be surprising for visitors: crowds so dense that traffic stops, around churches that most of the crowd does not belong to. It is friendly, loud and very photogenic. See where to go in our Christmas in Hanoi guide.

What do Vietnamese people eat at Christmas?

There is no national Christmas dish. Christmas arrived through the Church and the French, not through the farming calendar like Tết, so it has no equivalent of bánh chưng.

  • Bûche de Noël: the French yule log cake, called bánh khúc cây, is the most visible Christmas food. Bakeries start selling it from mid-November. Back in 2010, a single big bakery chain reportedly made 140,000 of them.
  • Hot pot with friends: in cold Hanoi, a shared lẩu is a common Christmas Eve dinner.
  • Grilled street snacks: sold around churches and squares on Christmas Eve.
  • Hotel buffets: big hotels sell Western-style Christmas dinners with turkey, ham and roast meats.

Where Christmas is biggest in Vietnam

  • Hanoi: St. Joseph's Cathedral, Hàng Mã Street and the lake. Cool weather makes it feel the most "wintery."
  • Ho Chi Minh City: Notre-Dame Cathedral Saigon, Nguyễn Huệ walking street and the big malls. Warm and very crowded.
  • Đà Lạt and Da Nang: both have well-known cathedrals nicknamed the "Rooster Church" that draw big Christmas Eve crowds.
  • Catholic heartlands: parishes in Ninh Bình, Nam Định and Đồng Nai, where whole villages decorate.
  • Sa Pa: in 2025 the town opened a Snow Festival around Christmas.

Visitor etiquette

  • Cover shoulders and knees inside churches.
  • Don't use flash or walk around during Mass.
  • Ask before photographing people at prayer.
  • Expect crowds and keep valuables in front pockets.
  • A simple "Giáng sinh vui vẻ" to your hotel staff goes a long way.

Frequently asked questions

Is Christmas a public holiday in Vietnam?

No. December 25 is a normal working day. Some international schools and embassies close.

Do Vietnamese people give Christmas gifts?

Many families give small gifts to children, and couples and friends often exchange presents. It is lighter than the gift-giving at Tết.

What is the best place to spend Christmas in Vietnam?

Hanoi for cool weather and old-world atmosphere, Ho Chi Minh City for big crowds and warm nights, and Phú Quốc if you want a beach. See Vietnam in December for the weather by region.

Is everything open on Christmas Day in Vietnam?

Yes, almost everything. Shops, restaurants, sights and transport run as normal.
