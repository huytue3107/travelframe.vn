Hang Ma Street at Christmas: Hanoi's Most Photogenic Holiday Street + 7 Nearby Photo Spots

Every December, Hanoi's festival-supply street turns into a tunnel of tinsel, trees and Santas. When to go, how to shoot it politely and a short walking route to seven more holiday photo spots.

Hàng Mã means "street of paper goods." For centuries it sold the paper offerings Vietnamese families burn for their ancestors. Today it sells whatever the next festival needs: lanterns for Mid-Autumn, masks for Halloween and, in December, a wall-to-wall explosion of Christmas.

From the end of November, shopfronts disappear behind tinsel, plastic trees, LED snowflakes and life-size Santas. It is loud, cheerful and very photogenic. Here's how to get good photos without getting in anyone's way, plus a short walk to seven more holiday spots.

Hàng Mã at Christmas: the essentials

  • Where: Hàng Mã Street in the north of the Old Quarter, about a 10-minute walk from Hoàn Kiếm Lake. The decorations spill onto nearby Lương Văn Can and Hàng Lược.
  • When: Decorations go up from late November. In 2025, some shops started in early November. They stay up until just after Christmas, then switch to New Year and Tết stock.
  • Cost: Free to walk. Some shops now ask 25,000–30,000 VND per person to take photos inside, according to local press in December 2025.
  • Prices: Small ornaments start around 20,000 VND. Big trees cost millions of dong.

When to go

  • Weekday, 4–5:30pm: the best balance. Shop lights are on, there's still daylight on faces and the street is moving.
  • After dark, 6–9pm: the full glow, but also the biggest crowds and plenty of motorbikes.
  • Christmas Eve: fun to see, hard to photograph. In 2025, local press reported the streets around Hàng Mã, the Opera House and the lake jammed for hours.
  • Morning: quiet, but many shops open slowly and the lights are off.

How to photograph Hàng Mã (politely)

  1. Buy something small first. A bauble or a Santa hat costs little and turns you from a blocker into a customer.
  2. Ask before shooting inside. A smile and a pointed phone are enough. If there's a sign about a photo fee, pay it.
  3. Don't block doorways or the road. Hàng Mã is a working street. Motorbikes keep moving even on busy nights.
  4. Use the shop as your light. Stand facing the shop, with your back to the street. The display lights your face and fills the background.
  5. Shoot through the tinsel. Hold your phone close to a garland so it blurs in the foreground. It instantly looks festive.
  6. Turn off the flash. It flattens the lights and annoys shopkeepers.

7 more Christmas photo spots within walking distance

This route takes about 2 hours on foot with stops. Walking times are approximate.

1. Lương Văn Can Street (2 minutes away)

Hanoi's toy street runs just south of Hàng Mã. In December, its shops add trees and decorations too, but with fewer people stopping to pose.

2. St. Joseph's Cathedral (about 12 minutes)

The big Christmas tree and nativity scene go up in the square in December. Come at 3:30–5pm for soft light on the stone. Full details in our St. Joseph's Cathedral guide.

3. Hoàn Kiếm Lake and the Thê Húc Bridge (about 5 minutes)

The red bridge to Ngọc Sơn Temple glows after sunset. Shoot from the shore on Đinh Tiên Hoàng Street during blue hour, around 5:20–6pm in December.

4. Tràng Tiền Plaza (about 8 minutes)

The department store at the south end of the lake usually wraps its whole facade in lights. Stand on the opposite corner for the full building.

5. The Opera House square (about 5 minutes)

In recent years the square in front of the Hanoi Opera House has hosted large seasonal light displays around New Year. The colonial facade is a classic backdrop any evening. See our French Quarter guide.

6. Cửa Bắc Church (a short taxi ride)

This 1930s church at 56 Phan Đình Phùng, next to the old citadel's north gate, decorates for Christmas with far smaller crowds than the cathedral.

7. A decorated café with a view

Many Old Quarter cafés put up trees and lights in December. Pick one with a balcony over the street, order a hot drink and shoot the crowd below. For coffee ideas, see our Old Quarter food guide.

Frequently asked questions

When does Hàng Mã decorate for Christmas?

Usually from late November until just after December 25. Some shops start in early November.

Is Hàng Mã free to visit?

Yes. Walking the street is free. Some shops charge a small fee to take photos inside, so ask first.

What is the best time to take photos on Hàng Mã?

A weekday between 4 and 5:30pm, when the shop lights are on and the crowd is still manageable.

Can I buy Christmas decorations on Hàng Mã?

Yes. It is Hanoi's main market for holiday decorations, from small ornaments to full-size trees. Prices are usually marked or quick to ask: bao nhiêu tiền? means "how much?"
