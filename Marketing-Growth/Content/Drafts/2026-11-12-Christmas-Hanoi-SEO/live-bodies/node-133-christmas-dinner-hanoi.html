Christmas Dinner and Cozy Winter Eats in Hanoi (December 24–25)

Christmas Eve is Hanoi's big night out, and tables go fast. How hotel Christmas dinners work, the local winter dishes worth trying instead and where to eat late after the cathedral crowds.

In Vietnam, Christmas is a night, not a day. The big meal happens on the evening of December 24, before or after people head out to see the lights. On December 25, most Hanoians are back at work.

So if you want a special dinner, plan for Christmas Eve and book early. And if you'd rather eat like a local, December is the best month of the year for Hanoi's hot, steaming food.

Option 1: a hotel Christmas Eve dinner

Hanoi's big international hotels run Christmas Eve dinners, brunches and afternoon teas every year. For example, in 2025:

  • Sofitel Legend Metropole Hanoi ran special dining events across its restaurants and bars through the season, including Le Beaulieu and Spice Garden.
  • Mövenpick Hotel Hanoi Centre served a Christmas Eve dinner at its Mangosteen restaurant, with seafood, foie gras and smoked salmon.
  • Meliá Hanoi offered a daily Christmas afternoon tea from 3 to 5pm.

These are last year's examples. Hotels usually publish 2026 menus in late November or early December. A few tips:

  • Book early. Tables are limited on Christmas Eve.
  • Check the "++". Hotel prices are often quoted before service charge and VAT.
  • Ask about timing. An early seating lets you walk to the cathedral afterward. The French Quarter hotels are a 10–15 minute walk away.

Option 2: eat like a Hanoian in winter

Cold weather is when Hanoi's food is at its best. Here's what locals actually eat in December. Addresses and hours are in our where to eat in the Old Quarter guide.

Hot pot (lẩu)

The default dinner for a group of friends on a cold night, Christmas Eve included. A bubbling pot in the middle of the table, plates of beef, seafood, greens and noodles to cook yourself. Go with at least two people.

Chả cá

Turmeric fish fried at your table with dill and spring onions. Warm, fragrant and perfect for a festive dinner. Chả Cá Thăng Long, at 6B Đường Thành, is a classic.

Phở, early

Beef phở is a breakfast dish here. On a cold morning, a bowl at Phở Gia Truyền (49 Bát Đàn) is the best cure for a late Christmas Eve.

Egg coffee

Whipped egg yolk and sugar over strong coffee, often served in a bowl of hot water to keep it warm. Café Giảng, down the lane at 39 Nguyễn Hữu Huân, is where it started.

Hot sweet soups and street snacks

Warm chè (sweet soups), roasted chestnuts and grilled skewers appear on Old Quarter corners in winter. Around the cathedral on Christmas Eve, street vendors sell snacks to the crowd.

Dessert: the bûche de Noël

The one Christmas food every Vietnamese city shares is French. Bánh khúc cây, the yule log cake, fills bakery windows from mid-November. Pick up a small one for your hotel room or a homestay host. Read more about how Vietnam celebrates Christmas.

Eating late after midnight Mass

Christmas Eve crowds don't clear until after midnight. A few Old Quarter places serve late:

  • Phở Gà Nguyệt, 5B Phủ Doãn: chicken phở, close to the cathedral, open late according to its listings.
  • Xôi Yến, 35B Nguyễn Hữu Huân: hearty sticky rice, open until late according to its listings.
  • Tạ Hiện: the beer street stays busy with snacks well past midnight.

Hours change on holiday nights, so have a backup.

Vegetarian at Christmas

Ask for đồ chay (vegetarian food) or say ăn chay ("I eat vegetarian"). Many hot pot restaurants offer a mushroom or vegetable broth. Hotel buffets usually label vegetarian dishes.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to book Christmas dinner in Hanoi?

For hotels and popular restaurants on December 24, yes. Street food and casual spots don't take bookings, but they get busy after 7pm.

Are restaurants open on Christmas Day in Hanoi?

Yes. December 25 is a normal working day in Vietnam, so restaurants keep regular hours.

What do Vietnamese people eat on Christmas Eve?

There is no fixed dish. Catholic families eat a home meal after Mass, and many young people go out for hot pot or street food with friends. Yule log cakes are the most common Christmas treat.

What is a good warm dish to try in Hanoi in December?

Hot pot for dinner, phở for breakfast and egg coffee in the afternoon.
