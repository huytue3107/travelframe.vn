# Tet 2027 in Hanoi: Dates, What's Open and What to Do

Tet is Vietnam's Lunar New Year and the biggest holiday of the year. For about a week it changes how Hanoi works.

In 2027, Tet falls on **Saturday, February 6**. If you are in Hanoi in the first two weeks of February, the holiday will shape your trip. This guide covers what the city is like before, during and after Tet, and how to plan around it.

*Last checked: November 14, 2026. Hanoi has not announced its 2027 Tet events yet. Where that is the case, we say so and show what happened in 2026. We will update this page as the plans come out.*

## Quick answer

- **When is Tet 2027?** Lunar New Year's Day is Saturday, February 6, 2027. It starts the Year of the Goat.
- **How long is the holiday?** Public offices close from Thursday, February 4 to Wednesday, February 10. Work restarts on Thursday, February 11.
- **Is Hanoi closed?** Not fully. Many family shops and street food stalls close for several days. Hotels stay open, and in 2026 several of the main sights did too. Some Old Quarter restaurants usually stay open, so ask ahead.
- **Best days for photos:** the week before Tet, for flower markets and red-and-gold streets. Then the morning of day 1, when the Old Quarter is at its quietest.
- **Busiest days to travel:** expect full roads, trains and buses around February 4 and February 10, when people travel home for the holiday and back to work.

Good to know: the last lunar month has only 29 days this time, so New Year's Eve is February 5. Many guides mention a 30th day, but there is none this year.

## What is Tet, in two minutes

Tet is short for *Tết Nguyên Đán*. It marks the first day of the lunar calendar and the start of spring.

Think of it as Christmas and New Year in one week, with everyone going home. People travel back to their home towns. They clean and decorate the house, cook special food, and visit relatives, teachers and friends.

For visitors, the days before Tet are busy and colourful, and they are the best time for photos. The first days of the new year are quiet and kept for family.

## Is Hanoi closed during Tet?

Partly.

### Usually closed for a few days

- Small family restaurants and most street food stalls.
- Local markets and many shops in the Old Quarter.
- Banks and government offices, from February 4 to 10.
- Some museums and smaller sights. Closing days differ, so check each one before you go.

### Usually open

- Hotels, including hotel restaurants.
- Some restaurants and cafes in the Old Quarter that serve travelers. Expect fewer choices than normal, and ask about a holiday surcharge.
- Temples and pagodas, which are busy all week.
- Hoan Kiem Lake and the streets around it.

### What happened last year

Hanoi is busy with visitors at Tet. During Tet 2026 the city counted about 1.34 million visitors in nine days, a record, and about 217,000 of them came from abroad.

The Temple of Literature stayed open through the holiday in 2026. It closed only on the afternoon of New Year's Eve, and it was the most visited site in the city that week.

The city also made a group of heritage sites free to enter for a few days late in the holiday. The list included the Thang Long Citadel and Hoa Lo Prison.

As of November 2026, the city has not published its 2027 plans. We will update this page when it does.

**Pro move:** ask your hotel in January which nearby restaurants stay open. Then save two or three names before New Year's Eve.

## The week before Tet: the best time for photos

If you can choose your dates, arrive before the holiday. Hanoi looks its best from late January to February 5.

**Flower markets.** Peach blossom is the flower of Tet in the north. Kumquat trees ride through the streets on the back of motorbikes.

- *Hang Luoc flower market* in the Old Quarter is held once a year. By tradition it runs from Kitchen Gods' Day (January 30 in 2027) until New Year's Eve. The 2027 dates are not announced yet.
- *Quang Ba flower market* (236 Au Co, Tay Ho) is a wholesale night market. It is busiest between about 1am and 3am.
- *Nhat Tan* is the peach-growing area near West Lake, and a popular place for photos among the trees.

**Red and gold streets.** Hang Ma Street sells Tet decorations: lanterns, red envelopes and paper banners. We wrote about [photographing Hang Ma Street](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hang-ma-street-christmas-photo-spots) at Christmas, and the same advice on light and crowds applies.

**Calligraphy at the Temple of Literature.** Around Tet, calligraphers write lucky words on red paper beside the temple lake. In 2026 the Spring Calligraphy Festival ran from February 11 to March 1 with 35 stalls. The 2027 dates are not announced yet.

**Pro move:** at any market, keep your camera small and step out of the way, because people are working. Ask first before a close-up portrait.

## New Year's Eve in Hanoi (Friday, February 5)

By late afternoon most shops are shut and families are home for the last dinner of the year.

Around Hoan Kiem Lake, people gather before midnight. In 2026 Hanoi set off fireworks for 15 minutes from midnight at 33 locations, including the lake area. The 2027 plan is not announced yet.

- Eat early. Book dinner at your hotel, or at a restaurant that confirms it is open.
- Walk if you can. Streets near the lake can close to traffic, and ride-hailing is slow.
- Dress warm. It is a long, damp night outside.

Do not confuse this night with December 31. For that one, see our [New Year's Eve in Hanoi guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/new-years-eve-hanoi).

## Days 1 to 3: a quiet Hanoi

On the morning of day 1 the Old Quarter is as empty as you will ever see it. Shutters are down, and a few people walk to the pagoda in their best clothes.

What works well on these days:

- **A slow walk through the Old Quarter.** Start at Hoan Kiem Lake and wander. Our [Old Quarter guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hanoi-old-quarter-guide) has a simple route.
- **Temples and pagodas.** Ngoc Son Temple on the lake and Tran Quoc Pagoda on West Lake fill with families making their first visit of the year.
- **The Temple of Literature.** Students and parents come to ask for good luck in exams.
- **Hotel breakfast, then a long coffee.** Cafes reopen on different days, so take the one you find open.

What does not work well: food tours, shopping, and anything that needs a small local business to be open.

We could not confirm the Tet opening days of the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum. Check the official notice before you go.

## Tet etiquette for visitors

You do not need to know every custom. These five cover most situations.

- **Say it.** *Chúc mừng năm mới* means Happy New Year. It is worth learning before you arrive.
- **Do not drop in.** The first visitor of the year is believed to bring the family's luck, a custom called *xông đất*. Do not enter a home on day 1 unless you are invited.
- **Red envelopes.** Adults give children lucky money in red envelopes. In the north it is called *mừng tuổi*. If a family invites you, a small new note for each child is a kind gesture.
- **Dress well.** People wear new clothes or the *áo dài*, Vietnam's traditional dress. Bright colours are welcome. At temples, cover shoulders and knees.
- **Ask first.** Tet is family time. Ask before you photograph people, altars or prayers.

## Getting around, money and weather

**Transport.** Many people travel home for Tet, so trains, buses and flights around the holiday can sell out. If you must move between cities, book early.

**Ride-hailing.** Rides can take longer to find over the holiday. Grab added a Tet surcharge from February 16 to 20 in 2026. The 2027 surcharge is not announced yet, so check the fare in the app before you confirm.

**Money.** Banks close for the holiday, so take out the cash you need before February 4.

**Prices.** Restaurants that stay open may add a Tet surcharge. Look for it on the menu, or ask first.

**Weather.** Early February is cool and often grey. Daytime highs are around 21°C and nights around 16°C, with light drizzle on some days. Bring a jacket and shoes that handle wet pavement.

## Should you visit Hanoi during Tet?

Tet suits you if you want to watch a city get ready for its biggest holiday, you like quiet streets and slow mornings, and you care more about atmosphere than about ticking off sights.

It is a poor fit if you have only two days in Hanoi and a long must-eat list, if you need to cross the country by train or bus that week, or if you came for nightlife and shopping.

For most travelers the best plan is to arrive two or three days before February 6 and stay until day 3 or 4.

## A simple 4-day Tet plan

- **Thu, Feb 4.** Old Quarter walk in the morning. Hang Ma Street in the afternoon, plus the Hang Luoc flower market if it is running.
- **Fri, Feb 5.** Temple of Literature in the morning. In 2026 it closed at noon on this day. Early dinner. Hoan Kiem Lake for midnight.
- **Sat, Feb 6.** Sleep in. Walk the empty Old Quarter. Visit Ngoc Son Temple and Tran Quoc Pagoda.
- **Sun, Feb 7.** West Lake on foot or by bicycle. A long coffee. Start checking which places have reopened.

## FAQ

**When is Tet in 2027?**
Lunar New Year's Day is Saturday, February 6, 2027. New Year's Eve is Friday, February 5.

**How long is the Tet holiday in Vietnam in 2027?**
The public-sector holiday is seven days, from February 4 to February 10. Private businesses choose their own five days, so some shops close a day earlier or later.

**Is everything closed in Hanoi during Tet?**
No. Many small shops and street food stalls close for several days. Hotels and temples stay open, and in 2026 several of the main sights did too. Some Old Quarter restaurants usually stay open, so ask ahead.

**Is Tet a good time to visit Hanoi?**
Yes, if you come for atmosphere and photos and you plan your meals. Arrive before February 6 to see the markets.

**What animal is 2027 in the Vietnamese zodiac?**
The Goat. The Year of the Horse ends on February 5, 2027.

**How do you say Happy New Year in Vietnamese?**
*Chúc mừng năm mới.*

**What should I wear in Hanoi at Tet?**
A warm layer and comfortable shoes. For photos, red, yellow and other bright colours fit the season.
