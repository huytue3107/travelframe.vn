Hanoi's Old Quarter is the tight grid of streets just north of Hoan Kiem Lake. For centuries it was the city's merchant district, and it is still where most visitors sleep, eat and spend their first day. The first hour can feel like a lot: motorbikes, shopfronts spilling onto the pavement, smells of grilled pork and incense. Give it a morning and it turns into one of the most walkable neighborhoods in Asia.

This guide is written by Hanoi locals. It covers how the quarter is laid out, the things actually worth your time, a simple one-day plan, the weekend night market, where to stay, how to get here from the airport, and where to take the best photos.

A simple mental map of the Old Quarter

Think of the Old Quarter as a rough triangle:

  • South: Hoan Kiem Lake. The easiest landmark, and where most walks begin.
  • North: Dong Xuan Market, the big covered market at the top of Hang Dao and Hang Ngang.
  • East: the Long Bien Bridge approach and the dyke road along the Red River. O Quan Chuong, the old city gate, sits on this side.
  • West: Phung Hung street and its brick railway arches.

Many streets still carry the name of the trade once practiced there. Hang means "goods": Hang Bac was silver, Hang Ma paper offerings, Hang Thiec tin, Hang Buom sails. A few streets still sell what their name promises, which is half the fun of walking here. Locals call the area "36 streets", although there are more than that today.

Map tip: Save a few pins before you go: your hotel, Hoan Kiem Lake, Dong Xuan Market, St. Joseph's Cathedral and O Quan Chuong gate. Street names change at almost every corner, so an app with offline maps is more useful than a paper map. If you lose your bearings, walk until you reach the lake and start again.

10 best things to do in the Hanoi Old Quarter

1. Walk around Hoan Kiem Lake at sunrise

From first light, locals jog, dance and practice tai chi around the lake. Cross the red The Huc Bridge to Ngoc Son Temple before the tour groups arrive. Read more about Ngoc Son Temple.

2. Step inside a merchant's tube house at 87 Ma May

Old Quarter houses are narrow at the front and run deep into the block, with small courtyards to let in light and air. The restored heritage house at 87 Ma May shows how a merchant family lived and traded under one roof. Entry costs around 20,000 VND; check the current fee at the door.

3. Visit Bach Ma Temple

At 76 Hang Buom, Bach Ma ("White Horse") Temple is one of the oldest temples in the quarter. Legend says a white horse showed the king where to build the city walls. It is free to enter and usually opens in the morning and mid-afternoon, with a break at lunch. Dress modestly.

4. Find O Quan Chuong, the last old city gate

Thang Long once had several gates. O Quan Chuong, first built in 1749 and rebuilt in the early 1800s, is the only one still standing. Motorbikes still pass through it, which makes it a great spot to watch the city move.

5. Get lost in the small lanes

The main streets are busy, but narrow alleys open into courtyards, family shrines and tiny cafés. Start with our 5 Old Quarter alleys to slow down in.

6. Drink egg coffee where it was invented

Egg coffee is a Hanoi original, whipped egg yolk and sugar over strong coffee. Read the story of Café Giảng and how to photograph it.

7. Explore Dong Xuan Market and Hang Ma street

Dong Xuan is the city's largest covered market and sells mostly wholesale goods: fabric, household items, dried foods. The food stalls around its edges are the real draw. Nearby, Hang Ma glows with paper lanterns and offerings, especially in the weeks before Mid-Autumn Festival and Lunar New Year.

8. See St. Joseph's Cathedral

The neo-Gothic St. Joseph's Cathedral stands on the quarter's southwest edge, with cafés and lemon-tea stalls on the steps around it.

9. Spend an evening on Ta Hien

Ta Hien and the streets around it fill with low plastic stools, cold beer and music after dark. Our guide: Ta Hien street, where Hanoi nights come alive.

10. Walk out to the railway and Long Bien Bridge

Hanoi's railway history runs along the quarter's edges. Read our Train Street safety guide before you go, and consider the calmer view from Long Bien Bridge at sunset.

One perfect day in the Old Quarter

Morning

  • 6:00–7:30: Hoan Kiem Lake and The Huc Bridge while the city exercises.
  • 7:30: Breakfast phở or bánh cuốn at a small shop with stools on the pavement.
  • 8:30–11:00: 87 Ma May, Bach Ma Temple and the lanes around Hang Buom and Ma May.

Afternoon

  • Lunch: bún chả. See our bún chả guide.
  • Egg coffee break, then Dong Xuan Market and Hang Ma.
  • Late afternoon: O Quan Chuong gate, then walk toward Long Bien Bridge for sunset.

Evening

  • Chả cá on its own street, or street food around Dong Xuan.
  • Friday to Sunday: the walking street and night market. Any night: a beer on Ta Hien.

Hanoi Old Quarter night market and walking street

From Friday evening to Sunday night, the streets around Hoan Kiem Lake close to traffic and become a walking street, with families, street games and live music.

Inside the Old Quarter, the weekend night market runs along Hang Dao, Hang Ngang and Hang Duong, heading north toward Dong Xuan Market. Expect clothing, souvenirs, snacks and crowds.

  • When: Friday to Sunday, from around 7 pm until about midnight. Times can shift, so check with your hotel on the day.
  • Tips: Go early in the evening before it gets packed. Keep your phone in a front pocket, and bargain politely at clothing stalls.

Hanoi Old Quarter photos: where and when to shoot

Yellow facades, green shutters, tangled power lines, lanterns and shopfronts make the Old Quarter Hanoi's most photogenic neighborhood.

  • Early morning for empty streets and soft light around the lake.
  • Late afternoon for warm light on Hang Ma lanterns and the old gate.
  • Blue hour for shop lights and motorbike trails, shot from the pavement or a café upstairs.

Where to stay in or near the Old Quarter

Staying inside or on the edge of the Old Quarter puts you within walking distance of the lake, the food and the night market. Pick your area by how much noise you can live with.

How to get from Hanoi airport to the Old Quarter

Noi Bai International Airport is roughly 30 km north of the Old Quarter. Allow 45–60 minutes by car, more at rush hour.

  • Ride-hailing (Grab, Xanh SM): the simplest door-to-door option. Check the fare in the app before you confirm.
  • Airport bus 86: leaves from outside the arrivals area and runs to Hanoi Railway Station, passing near the Old Quarter and Hoan Kiem Lake. It has a flat fare of about 45,000 VND and runs roughly every 30 minutes from early morning to around 10 pm. Check the current timetable before you fly.
  • City bus 17: the cheapest option, to Long Bien bus station on the Old Quarter's edge. It is slower and makes more stops.
  • Hotel transfer: many Old Quarter hotels arrange pickup. Confirm the price in writing.

Practical tips

  • Crossing the street: walk at a steady pace and don't stop suddenly. Our guide to crossing the street in Hanoi explains how.
  • Pavements are parking lots. Expect to walk in the road at times. Keep to the edge.
  • Dress for temples: cover shoulders and knees for Ngoc Son, Bach Ma and other temples.
  • Cash helps: many small food stalls prefer cash. Carry small notes.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Hanoi Old Quarter?

One full day covers the highlights. Two days let you slow down, add the French Quarter and enjoy more evenings.

Is the Hanoi Old Quarter safe?

It is generally safe for visitors, including solo travelers. The main risks are traffic and petty theft in crowds. Watch your phone and bag at night markets.

What is the best time of day to explore the Old Quarter?

Early morning, for quiet streets and soft light around the lake, and early evening, when the food stalls open.

Is the Old Quarter walkable?

Yes. Hoan Kiem Lake to Dong Xuan Market is about a 15-minute walk without stops, and most sights in this guide are within 20 minutes of each other on foot.

When is the Hanoi Old Quarter night market open?

Friday to Sunday evenings, from around 7 pm until about midnight, on Hang Dao, Hang Ngang and Hang Duong.

Is it better to stay in the Old Quarter or the French Quarter?

Stay in the Old Quarter for food and atmosphere on your doorstep. Choose the French Quarter if you want quieter nights and are happy to walk 10–15 minutes in.
