# A Hanoi street style photoshoot with Harry

Wear something you love. Explore Hanoi with Harry behind the camera, a little posing guidance, and time to enjoy the city around you. Hanna's portraits at Long Bien Bridge offer a starting point for your own fashion-inspired photo walk.

![Hanna in a white dress adjusting her glasses beside the steel structure of Long Bien Bridge](../../../../Creative-Production/Assets/Raw/Hanna-Long-Bien-2026-10-22/hanna-long-bien-street-style-02.jpg)

*Hanna at Long Bien Bridge. Photograph from Harry's Instagram series, directed by 102 Koncept.*

## Hanna's Hanoi walk, through Harry's lens

In this set, Hanoi becomes part of the photograph. Rust-colored steel frames a white dress. A wider shot opens up the bridge around Hanna. A closer portrait brings her expression and the shape of her pose forward.

The series stays with one outfit and one recognizable setting. The photographs change through framing, body position, and where Hanna looks. It is a useful way to explain the street style experience: you bring your own look, and the photographer helps you find different ways to wear it in the city.

You can use her pictures as a starting point for your own session. Pick the ones that feel closest to your style. Harry and the TravelFrame team can discuss how to shape a walk around the photographs you want to bring home.

## A little direction helps you find your pose

You can ask for guidance throughout the shoot. A small change in where you look, how you stand, or the position of your hands can give you something clear to try.

In Hanna's close portrait, her arms create the shape of the frame as she leans toward the camera. In the wider photograph, a turned shoulder changes how she sits within the bridge's lines. Small movements give a pose something specific to work with.

TravelFrame's [behind-the-scenes posing video](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeZLzbzkWDx/) shares simple prompts such as leaning toward the camera, playing with your hair, and turning your back.

For your own walk, tell Harry how much direction you would like. Bring a reference picture if it helps explain the mood. You can also look through frames together and point out what you like.

## Leave room to experience the city

For your own session, leave time to notice Hanoi around you. The walk is part of the memory you are making.

Hanna's pictures keep the steel structure and the city beyond it in view. They give the portraits a connection to the place. Your own walk can begin with what interests you about Hanoi and the kind of setting you want in your photographs.

Tell us about the architecture, street scenes, or everyday details you would like to explore. We will discuss which locations suit your plans. This Long Bien series is one chapter to take inspiration from; your route is agreed for your session.

When a photograph includes another person, ask first. Keep entrances and pavements clear, and follow any rules at the location. Stay off railway tracks and follow local security instructions.

## Bring home a personal set of portraits

The photographs give you a way to remember yourself in Hanoi. Use Hanna's finished pictures to talk through the style you want for your own set.

You might prefer a close portrait or a wider frame that keeps more of the city in view. Share those preferences before the session so the conversation starts with pictures you like.

The selected portraits are edited for delivery. Ask to see examples of the finished look when planning your session. Harry can discuss the style with you before the shoot so the reference pictures and the final edit have a shared direction.
