### [PART 2/6: THE 1946 WARTIME INVENTION]
The French Metropole Origins:

In 1946, during the First Indochina War, fresh cow milk and condensed milk became scarce luxuries in Hanoi.

Mr Nguyen Van Giang, then a head barista at the historic French Metropole Hotel, looked for a creative way to create a rich cappuccino style foam.

He took fresh chicken egg yolks and whipped them vigorously with sweetened condensed milk and sugar. The creamy result became an instant legend.

---

### [PART 3/6: THE HOT WATER BOWL]
Why it arrives in a bath of hot water:

When a traditional cup of egg coffee is placed on your table, you will notice the glass sits inside a small porcelain bowl filled with steaming hot water.

This is not decorative.

The hot water bath acts as a gentle double boiler ("bain marie"), keeping the whipped egg yolk custard warm from below and ensuring zero eggy odor or curdling.

---

### [PART 4/6: THE 2 STEP SIP TECHNIQUE]
How to drink it like a local connoisseur:

Step 1: Use your tiny spoon to taste two spoonfuls of the top custard foam on its own. It should taste velvety, sweet, and featherlight, like warm liquid tiramisu or fresh sabayon.

Step 2: Do NOT stir. Tilt the glass slightly so the piping hot, bitter robusta coffee underneath flows directly through the sweet cream layer into your mouth. The hot bitter and cool sweet contrast is pure genius.

---

### [PART 5/6: WHERE TO FIND THE ORIGINALS]
The two heritage spots to visit:

(1) Cafe Giang (39 Nguyen Huu Huan): Founded by Mr Giang himself, run by his descendants in a deep, labyrinth alley.

(2) Cafe Dinh (13 Dinh Tien Hoang): Opened by his daughter, Mrs Bich. Hidden on the second floor above an old backpack store with a rustic French balcony overlooking Hoan Kiem lake.
