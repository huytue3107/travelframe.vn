---

### [PART 2/5: THE NO TIPPING BASELINE]
The General Rule: Vietnam is NOT a tipping country.

Locals do not tip at street noodle stalls, neighborhood coffee counters, fruit carts, or casual restaurants.

Prices listed on wall menus are final and all inclusive.

In fact, if you leave a 50,000 VND note on a blue plastic stool and walk away, the server will often sprint down the sidewalk after you to hand back your "forgotten money."

---

### [PART 3/5: SERVICE CHARGES ON BILLS]
Check the bottom of your restaurant receipt:

At mid to high end dining spots and cocktail lounges, your bill will often include a 5% Service Charge along with standard government VAT (8% or 10%).

That 5% service charge is distributed directly to staff. You do not need to add another tip on top. Simply round up the change if the service was exceptional.

---

### [PART 4/5: 3 TIMES WHEN TIPPING IS WELCOMED]
When tipping is customary and genuinely appreciated:

(1) Spa and Massage Therapists: Tipping 50,000 to 100,000 VND ($2 to $4 USD) for an hour of hard physical labor is customary.
(2) Private Day Guides and Drivers: Leaving $5 to $10 USD per person after a full day mountain excursion is standard.
(3) Ride Hailing Apps: Rounding up Grab fares by 10,000 to 20,000 VND during torrential rainstorms is deeply appreciated.
