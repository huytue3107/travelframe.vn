### [PART 1/5: ROOT HOOK]
IS ICE IN VIETNAM ACTUALLY SAFE TO DRINK, OR ARE YOU GUARANTEED A RUINED TRIP?

Almost every outdated guidebook warns travelers with the same terrifying blanket advice: "Never touch ice in Southeast Asia under any circumstances."

As a result, first time visitors sit in 35 degree humid heat sipping warm soda or refusing iced coffee out of pure fear.

Here is the real ground truth about ice in Hanoi:

---

### [PART 2/5: THE HOLLOW CYLINDER RULE]
Look at the shape of the ice in your glass:

(1) The Hollow Cylinder ("Da Ong"): If the ice has a neat cylindrical shape with a clean hole through the middle, it comes from an industrial factory using deep drilled purified water and modern freezing machines.

This ice is 100% safe to consume. Over 98% of cafes, juice bars, and street tea stalls in Hanoi buy bags of this certified tubular ice delivered fresh daily.

---

### [PART 3/5: THE RAGGED CRUSHED ICE TO AVOID]
The one type of ice you should skip:

(2) Ragged Block Shavings ("Da Tang"): If the ice consists of jagged, irregular shards chipped with an ice pick from massive 50 kilogram blocks delivered on burlap mats, be cautious.

These industrial blocks are intended for chilling seafood and crates of beer at wholesale wet markets, not for drinking. If you see chunks chipped off a burlap sack on the curb, skip it.

---

### [PART 4/5: LOCAL STALL SANITATION]
Hanoi locals are just as mindful about food hygiene as you are.

Street vendors drink the exact same iced green tea ("Tra Da") and fruit smoothies with their families all day long. Nobody wants an upset stomach or lost business.

Order your iced coconut coffee, fresh sugarcane juice, or cold jasmine tea without anxiety. Just take a quick glance into your cup to check for clean hollow cylinders.
