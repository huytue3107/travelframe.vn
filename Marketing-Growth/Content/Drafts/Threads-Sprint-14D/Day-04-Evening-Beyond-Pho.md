### [PART 1/7: ROOT HOOK]
6 THINGS TO EAT IN HANOI THAT AREN'T PHO, AND MOST FIRST TIMERS MISS 5 OF THEM.

Pho is legendary. We eat it for breakfast several times a week.

However, if you come to Hanoi and only eat pho every single day, you are missing 90% of the deepest culinary magic this 1000 year old city has to offer.

Here are 6 iconic dishes to add to your map right now:

---

### [PART 2/7: DISH 1 — BUN CHA]
(1) Bun Cha (Smoky Grilled Pork Meatballs with Rice Vermicelli).

Forget sterile sit down tourist spots. Look for a tiny alley where smoke billows from a steel wire basket of pork patties grilling over red hot charcoal.

You get a bowl of warm, amber fish sauce vinegar broth loaded with crispy patties, pickled papaya, fresh herbs, and vermicelli. The smokiness is unreal.

---

### [PART 3/7: DISH 2 — BANH CUON]
(2) Banh Cuon (Steamed Rice Rolls).

Watch the cook spread fermented rice batter over a stretched silk cloth over boiling steam. In 15 seconds, she lifts a paper thin sheet, fills it with minced pork and wood ear mushrooms, and rolls it up.

Topped with crispy fried shallots and dipped in warm dipping sauce with pork roll, it melts in your mouth like silk.

---

### [PART 4/7: DISH 3 — CHA CA]
(3) Cha Ca (Turmeric Catfish Sizzling with Fresh Dill).

This is a theatrical feast. A sizzling cast iron skillet arrives at your table with chunks of marinated turmeric fish.

The server tosses in giant handfuls of fresh dill and green scallions. They wilt down in the bubbling oil in seconds. You eat it over vermicelli with roasted peanuts and shrimp paste.

---

### [PART 5/7: DISH 4 — BUN DAU MAM TOM]
(4) Bun Dau Mam Tom (Crispy Tofu and Fermented Shrimp Paste).

This is the ultimate local badge of honor. A round bamboo platter with golden fried tofu squares, boiled pork belly, and green rice sausage.

The dipping sauce is dark purple fermented shrimp paste whisked with lime juice and chili until frothy. If the smell intimidates you, ask for sweet garlic fish sauce instead.

---

### [PART 6/7: DISH 5 & 6 — NOM BO KHO & CA PHE TRUNG]
(5) Nom Bo Kho (Sweet and Sour Dried Beef Salad).
Shredded green papaya, fragrant Vietnamese mint, roasted crushed peanuts, and chewy spiced beef jerky tossed in chili lime vinegar. The crunchiest afternoon snack in the Old Quarter.

(6) Ca Phe Trung (Warm Egg Coffee).
Whisked egg yolks with sweetened condensed milk poured over rich robusta espresso. It tastes like warm liquid tiramisu in a cup.
