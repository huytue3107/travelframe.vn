### [PART 2/6: TUBE HOUSE ARCHITECTURE]
The Secret Architecture of Tube Houses:

In ancient Hanoi, properties were taxed based on street frontage width. Families built homes only three meters wide but up to 80 meters deep ("Nha Ong" or tube houses).

As you walk past the front parlor, the narrow corridor suddenly opens up into a luminous central skylight courtyard filled with potted ferns, mossy French bricks, and antique wooden birdcages.

---

### [PART 3/6: TWO ICONIC HIDDEN SANCTUMS]
Two legendary hidden courtyards to discover:

(1) Loading T (8 Chan Cam): Enter through a residential arched entry, climb a curving stone staircase under yellow peeling walls, and sit in a romantic vintage parlor smelling of roasted cinnamon and butter espresso.

(2) Dinh Cafe (13 Dinh Tien Hoang): Walk through an unassuming backpack store, navigate a dim three foot wide corridor, and climb wooden stairs to Mrs Bich's balcony overlooking Hoan Kiem lake.

---

### [PART 4/6: WHAT TO ORDER]
Signature drinks beyond traditional coffee:

Try "Ca Phe Muoi" (salted cream iced coffee). Dark, intense robusta topped with a thick layer of lightly salted whipping cream.

Or order a glass of "Ca Phe Trung" (hot egg custard coffee) or iced coconut coffee blended with fresh coconut milk until frosty.

Every sip tells a multi generational story.

---

### [PART 5/6: HOW TO SPOT THEM]
How to find these secret doorways:

Look closely at ground level for tiny hand painted wooden signs propped up against alley entrances.

Do not be afraid to walk in. Living room residents will simply smile, nod, and point upward toward the wooden stairs.

Hanoians take deep pride in these historic hidden sanctums.
