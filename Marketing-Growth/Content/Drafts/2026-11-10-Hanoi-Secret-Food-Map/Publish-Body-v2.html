Some of the best meals in Hanoi cost less than a coffee back home, and you eat them on a plastic stool a few centimeters off the pavement. The hard part is knowing where to sit down.

This map covers six street corners we keep going back to, from a sizzling steak-and-egg breakfast to paper-thin steamed rice rolls. Each spot has the address with a Google Maps link, the hours and what you will pay. Print it or save it to your phone before you go.

1. Bò Né Cột Điện (Branch 5): steak for breakfast

Breakfast here arrives as a screaming-hot iron skillet: flash-seared beef, liver pâté, seasoned butter and an egg that keeps frying at your table. Tear off a piece of warm baguette, break the yolk into the gravy and mop the pan clean.

Photo walk note: the French-colonial stone arches of the Hàng Đậu Water Tower are about three minutes away on foot. Soft morning light suits them best.

  • Address: 5 Nguyễn Trung Trực, Ba Đình
  • Hours: 07:00–11:30 and 17:30–21:00
  • Price: about 105,000 VND (~$4.00)

2. Phở Bò Gia Truyền Bát Đàn: queue, pay, carry

The Old Quarter's best-known queue-and-carry phở stall. You line up, pay at the wooden counter and carry your own bowl to a table. The clear beef broth is slow-simmered with roasted ginger and star anise.

Order phở tái nạm (rare and well-done beef) with two fried crullers, quẩy, to dip in the broth.

  • Address: 49 Bát Đàn, Hoàn Kiếm
  • Hours: 06:30–08:30 is the best window; go early
  • Price: 55,000–65,000 VND (~$2.30)

3. Bún Chả Hàng Quạt (Alley 74): lunch down a narrow alley

Follow the smoke down an alley about a meter wide, ducking under the washing lines. Pork patties are grilled over wood embers and served in warm fish-sauce broth with green papaya. Add garlic and fresh chili, then dip your rice noodles, elbow to elbow with office workers on their lunch break.

  • Address: Ngõ 74 Hàng Quạt, Hoàn Kiếm
  • Hours: 10:30–13:30, lunch only
  • Price: 50,000–60,000 VND (~$2.10)

4. Bánh Mì 25: the golden crackle

Warm baguettes with a thin, crackly crust, filled with homemade liver pâté, pickled daikon, cucumber and cilantro. While you wait, grab a salted caramel coconut coffee across the street.

  • Address: 25 Hàng Cá, Hoàn Kiếm
  • Hours: 07:00–19:00
  • Price: 35,000–45,000 VND (~$1.50)

5. Café Giảng: egg coffee since 1946

Café Giảng is known as the birthplace of Vietnamese egg coffee. Whipped egg custard sits on top of strong Robusta, a bit like a warm, drinkable tiramisu. Taste the foam with a spoon first, then stir gently from the bottom up.

  • Address: 39 Nguyễn Hữu Huân, Hoàn Kiếm
  • Hours: open daily; check Google Maps for today's hours
  • Price: about 35,000 VND (~$1.35)

6. Bánh Cuốn Bà Hoành: steamed rice rolls

Paper-thin rice sheets are steamed on a cloth stretched over a pot, then rolled around minced pork and wood ear mushrooms. They come topped with crispy shallots and a slice of cinnamon pork sausage, chả quế.

  • Address: 66 Tô Hiến Thành, Hai Bà Trưng
  • Hours: 06:30–12:00
  • Price: 45,000–55,000 VND (~$1.90)

Three unwritten rules of the Hanoi sidewalk

Trust the plastic stools

Low blue stools mean fast turnover. If motorbikes are parked two deep and locals are chatting loudly, the food was cooked fresh that morning.

Machine ice is the norm

The hollow, tube-shaped ice (đá bi) you see in central Hanoi comes from ice factories, not the tap. Most visitors drink it in iced tea and coffee without trouble.

The lime wipe

Squeeze a little lime onto a paper napkin and wipe your chopsticks and spoon before you eat. Plenty of Hanoians do it, and you will fit right in.

Questions travelers ask

What time should I go for street food breakfast in Hanoi?

Between 6:30 and 8:30. Phở stalls like Bát Đàn are busiest and freshest early, and some close by late morning.

How much cash do I need?

A full day of eating at these six spots costs roughly 350,000 VND (about $13.50) per person. Bring small notes; most stalls prefer cash.
