The áo dài is Vietnam's traditional long dress: a fitted tunic with front and back panels, worn over loose trousers. It moves with every step, which is why it photographs so well.

Our photographer Harry has shot it around Hanoi in four very different ways. Here are those four looks, what makes each one work, and how to plan your own áo dài photoshoot.

Quick answer

  • Where: Hoàn Kiếm Lake for calm backdrops, West Lake for pagoda views, Old Quarter lantern streets for color.
  • When: early morning by the lake, when it is quieter. After dark for the lake with its lights on.
  • Color: pick one your backdrop doesn't have.
  • Shoes: flat sandals or clean white sneakers. You will walk more than you think.

Where to shoot in Hanoi

  • Hoàn Kiếm Lake: water, old trees and the Turtle Tower. Go early, when the paths are calmer and the light is softer.
  • West Lake: wider water and the red tower of Trấn Quốc Pagoda. It is a working pagoda, so dress modestly and keep voices low.
  • Old Quarter lantern streets: lantern and decoration shops turn whole streets into color. Walk slowly and let your photographer find a gap in the traffic.
  • French-era streets: yellow walls, green shutters and wide pavements suit a more formal look.
  • Temples and pagodas: lovely backdrops, but they are places of worship. Ask before taking photos, and stay outside if the answer is no.

We don't shoot on the Train Street rails. It is a residential railway. See our Train Street safety guide.

How to move in an áo dài

Walk slowly. The front panel lifts and sways with each step. That movement is the photo.

Give your hands a job. A fan, a conical hat or a few flowers. It stops the stiff-arms look.

Look away from the camera. Over your shoulder, down at the lake, toward a lantern. It reads more natural than a straight-on smile.

Keep it clean. Change before a street food stop. Light fabric shows every drop.

Pro move: do one slow walk toward the camera at the start. It warms you up and gives your photographer the panel movement early.
