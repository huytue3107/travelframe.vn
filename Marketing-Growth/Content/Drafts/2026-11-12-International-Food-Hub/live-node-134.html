Need a Break from Pho? Where to Find International Food in Hanoi (2026)

Pizza, sushi, curry or Korean BBQ: where Hanoi eats when it needs a break from noodles, neighborhood by neighborhood.

Hanoi food is the reason many people come. It is also a lot of noodles.

Somewhere around day five, many travelers want something familiar. A pizza. A curry. Sushi. A plain salad that nobody had to explain.

Good news: Hanoi has real international food, not just tourist-menu spaghetti. The trick is knowing which neighborhood cooks what. This guide is a map, not a ranking. Each section points you to an area and a few places to start.

Two honest notes before you go:

  • Hours and menus move. We checked these places against several sources on November 12, 2026. Our team is visiting each one in person, and we will update this page with what we find. Check the restaurant's own page before a long ride.
  • Delivery is easy. If you are tired, the food section of the Grab app delivers from many of the restaurants below to your hotel.

Pizza and Italian in the Old Quarter

You do not need to leave the Old Quarter for good pizza.

  • Pizza 4P's Bảo Khánh, 11B Báo Khánh, Hoàn Kiếm. The city's best-known pizza name: a chain born in Vietnam, with a Japanese touch and its own cheese made in Da Lat. It gets busy at dinner.
  • Pizza Lovers, 7 Chợ Gạo, Hoàn Kiếm. A small Italian-style pizzeria inside the Old Quarter.
  • Pane e Vino, 3 Nguyễn Khắc Cần, Hoàn Kiếm. A long-running Italian restaurant near the Opera House, with a big Italian wine list.
  • Napolis Pizza, 66A Đào Duy Từ, Hoàn Kiếm. Wood-fired pizza in the north of the Old Quarter.

Pro move: go early. Popular pizza places fill up between 7pm and 8:30pm, and walk-ins wait.

Indian food: curry, pure veg and Jain

Hanoi's Indian restaurants cluster inside the Old Quarter, and many of them sit a few streets apart. Several kitchens are fully vegetarian, some cook Jain food on request, and some use halal meat.

Good starting points include Namaste Hanoi (46 Thợ Nhuộm) and MAAZI Old Quarter (57 Đường Thành).

Ordering Jain food: say "Jain" first, then ask for no onion, no garlic and no root vegetables. Vegan: ask for no ghee, no cream and no paneer.

Halal and Middle Eastern food

Halal options are fewer in Hanoi than in Ho Chi Minh City. Most are in or near the Old Quarter, around Al-Noor Mosque at 12 Hàng Lược, the only mosque in northern Vietnam.

You will find halal Vietnamese dishes, Malaysian and Singaporean food, Egyptian grills and halal-friendly Indian kitchens. Halal status varies: some restaurants show a certificate, others say they use no pork, lard or alcohol. Ask every time, and check our upcoming halal guide for details.

Japanese: Hanoi's quiet "Japantown"

The streets around Linh Lang, Kim Mã and Đào Tấn in Ba Dinh are where Hanoi's Japanese community eats. Sushi bars, ramen shops and izakaya sit side by side. Come after dark, when the lanterns are on.

For a special night out, several MICHELIN-listed Japanese counters sit closer to the center, in and around the French Quarter.

Korean: BBQ, fried chicken and late-night soup

Hanoi's Korean community lives west of the center, in two main areas:

  • Trung Hòa–Nhân Chính (Cầu Giấy and Thanh Xuân): an older Korean neighborhood with many restaurants, Korean supermarkets and Korean signs. Good for everyday BBQ and fried chicken.
  • My Dinh and The Manor "K-Town" (Nam Từ Liêm): about a kilometer of Korean restaurants along Đỗ Đình Thiện and Trần Văn Lai streets, busiest in the evening.

Getting there: take a Grab car. Allow around half an hour from the Old Quarter, more in rush hour.

Pro move: go hungry, and go as a group. Korean BBQ is made for sharing.

Vegetarian and vegan

Hanoi is easier for vegetarians than its street food suggests. Buddhist vegetarian restaurants, called quán chay, have fed the city for generations, and many Old Quarter restaurants now print vegan menus.

  • Hoang's Restaurant, 54 Hàng Buồm: Vietnamese food with a vegan menu.
  • Tung's Kitchen, 2 Hàng Bông: Vietnamese with vegetarian choices.
  • Indian pure-veg kitchens in the Old Quarter, for thali and dal.

Ask for chay (vegetarian) and say không nước mắm (no fish sauce) if you are strict.

Western food and brunch in West Lake

Tây Hồ (West Lake) is where many foreign residents live, and it has the widest choice of Western cafés and restaurants. Make it a half day: brunch, then a walk or bike ride along the lake.

Closer to the center, there are American-style diners and smokehouses in and around the Old Quarter, such as S&L's Diner on Đường Thành and The Moose & Roo Smokehouse on Hai Bà Trưng.

Make the break part of your day

The best trips balance both. Eat bun cha for lunch. Have pizza or sushi for dinner without guilt.

Frequently asked questions

Is there good Western food in Hanoi?

Yes. The Old Quarter and French Quarter have pizza, burgers and Italian food. West Lake (Tây Hồ) has the widest choice of Western cafés and restaurants.

Where is Korean Town in Hanoi?

Hanoi has two Korean areas, both west of the center: Trung Hòa–Nhân Chính, and My Dinh, where the "K-Town" strip runs along Đỗ Đình Thiện and Trần Văn Lai streets.

Where is the Japanese area in Hanoi?

Around Linh Lang, Kim Mã and Đào Tấn streets in Ba Dinh district.

Can I find halal food in Hanoi?

Yes, mostly in and around the Old Quarter, near Al-Noor Mosque on Hàng Lược. Ask each restaurant about its halal status.

Is Hanoi good for vegetarians?

Yes. Look for quán chay (vegetarian restaurants), Indian pure-veg kitchens and Old Quarter restaurants with vegan menus.
