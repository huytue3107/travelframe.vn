# Need a Break from Pho? Where to Find International Food in Hanoi (2026)

Hanoi food is the reason many people come. It is also a lot of noodles.

Somewhere around day five, a lot of travelers want something familiar. A pizza. A curry. Sushi. A plain salad that nobody had to explain.

Good news: Hanoi has real international food, not just tourist-menu spaghetti. The trick is knowing which neighborhood cooks what. This guide is a map, not a ranking. Each section points you to the area and a few starting places. Where we have a full guide, we link to it.

## Pizza and Italian in the Old Quarter

You do not need to leave the Old Quarter for good pizza.

- **Pizza 4P's Bảo Khánh**, 11B Báo Khánh: the city's best-known pizza name, a Vietnam-born chain with a Japanese twist that makes its own cheese in Da Lat. Expect a queue at dinner. [VERIFY hours, price]
- **Pizza Lovers**, 7 Chợ Gạo: a small pizzeria in the Old Quarter, run with an Italian chef. [VERIFY]
- **Pane e Vino**, 3 Nguyễn Khắc Cần: a long-running Italian restaurant near the Opera House, with a big Italian wine list. [VERIFY]
- **Napolis Pizza**, 66A Đào Duy Từ: wood-fired pizza, open late. [VERIFY]

**Pro move:** book Pizza 4P's online or go before 6pm. Walk-ins wait at peak times. [VERIFY booking]

## Indian food: curry, pure veg and South Indian

Hanoi's Indian restaurants cluster inside the Old Quarter. Many of them serve pure-vegetarian and Jain menus, and some are halal-friendly.

Starting points include MAAZI (57 Hàng Bông), RK Spice (6 Hàng Lược) and Raj Bhog (47 Lãn Ông). [VERIFY]

For the full list, with pure-veg, Jain and South Indian picks, see our [guide to Indian restaurants in Hanoi](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/indian-restaurants-hanoi).

## Halal and Middle Eastern food

Halal options are fewer in Hanoi than in Ho Chi Minh City. Most are in or near the Old Quarter, around the city's mosque at 12 Hàng Lược.

You will find halal Vietnamese food (beef pho, banh mi), Indian, Indonesian, and a few Middle Eastern and Turkish grills. [VERIFY]

Read our [halal food guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/halal-food-hanoi) before you go. It explains which places show a certificate and which are "no pork" by their own word.

## Japanese: Hanoi's quiet "Japantown"

The streets around **Linh Lang, Kim Mã and Đào Tấn** in Ba Dinh are where Hanoi's Japanese community eats. Sushi bars, ramen shops and izakaya sit side by side.

For a big night out, a few MICHELIN-listed counters sit closer to the center, in the French Quarter.

Our [omakase and sushi guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/omakase-sushi-hanoi) covers both, with prices, booking tips and counter etiquette.

## Korean: BBQ, fried chicken and late-night soup

Hanoi has a large Korean community, and it lives west of the center. Two areas matter:

- **Trung Hòa–Nhân Chính** (Cầu Giấy and Thanh Xuân): an older Korean neighborhood with hundreds of restaurants, shops and Korean signs. Good for everyday BBQ and fried chicken. [VERIFY]
- **My Dinh / The Manor "K-Town"** (Nam Từ Liêm): about a kilometer of Korean restaurants along Đỗ Đình Thiện and Trần Văn Lai streets. Busy in the evening, with Korean BBQ, fried chicken and street snacks. [VERIFY]

Prefer to stay central? A few Korean BBQ spots are near Hoan Kiem Lake, including Jinro BBQ (61A Lương Ngọc Quyến). [VERIFY still open]

**Getting there:** take a Grab car. Without heavy traffic, My Dinh is about 25–35 minutes from the Old Quarter. [VERIFY]

**Pro move:** go hungry and in a group. Korean BBQ is priced and served for sharing.

## Vegetarian and vegan

Hanoi is easier for vegetarians than its pork-heavy street food suggests. Buddhist vegetarian restaurants (*quán chay*) have been here for generations, and the Old Quarter has many traveler-friendly menus.

Places travelers often name:

- **Hoang's Restaurant**, 54 Hàng Buồm: Vietnamese food with a vegan menu. [VERIFY]
- **Tung's Kitchen**, 2 Hàng Bông: Vietnamese with vegetarian options. [VERIFY]
- **Veggie Castle**, Truc Bach area: a vegan buffet. [VERIFY address, still open]

Ask for *chay* (vegetarian) and say *không nước mắm* (no fish sauce) if you are strict.

## Western food and expat brunch in West Lake

**Tây Hồ (West Lake)** is where many foreign residents live. If you want a long brunch, a burger or a steak, it is a good half-day trip: eat, then walk or cycle the lake.

Closer to the center, American-style diners and smokehouses sit in and around the Old Quarter, for example S&L's Diner on Đường Thành and The Moose & Roo Smokehouse on Hai Bà Trưng. [VERIFY]

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there good Western food in Hanoi?
Yes. The Old Quarter and French Quarter have pizza, burgers and Italian food. West Lake (Tây Hồ) has the widest choice of Western cafés and restaurants.

### Where is Korean Town in Hanoi?
Hanoi has two Korean areas: Trung Hòa–Nhân Chính, and My Dinh, where the Đỗ Đình Thiện–Trần Văn Lai "K-Town" strip is. Both are west of the city center. [VERIFY]

### Where is the Japanese area in Hanoi?
Around Linh Lang, Kim Mã and Đào Tấn streets in Ba Dinh district.

### Can I find halal food in Hanoi?
Yes, mostly in and around the Old Quarter. See our [halal food guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/halal-food-hanoi) for certified and Muslim-friendly options.

### Is Hanoi good for vegetarians?
Yes. Look for *quán chay* (vegetarian restaurants), Indian pure-veg restaurants, and Old Quarter cafés with vegan menus.
