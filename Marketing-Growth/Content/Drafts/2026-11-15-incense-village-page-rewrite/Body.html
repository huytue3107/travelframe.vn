About 35 km south of Hanoi, a whole village dries its incense in the sun. On a clear morning, yards and lanes fill with fanned-out bundles of red and pink sticks.

Quảng Phú Cầu (Quang Phu Cau) is often called the Hanoi incense village. This guide covers when the sticks are out, how to get there, how to behave in a working village, and how to come home with good photos.

Quick answer

  • Where: about 35 km south of central Hanoi. The old Quảng Phú Cầu commune is now part of Ứng Thiên commune.
  • Getting there: about 1 hour each way by car, depending on traffic.
  • When: a dry, sunny day, mid-morning, once the bundles are out. When it rains, the sticks stay inside.
  • Cost: the village lanes are free. Some yards and viewing platforms charge a small fee, so bring small notes.
  • Etiquette: this is a workplace. Ask before you step into a yard, and never step on the bundles.

What the village makes

Families here have made incense for more than a century. The bamboo comes from other provinces, such as Thanh Hóa, Nghệ An and Tuyên Quang.

Photo: Vietnam Hidden Light / Pexels

The bamboo is split into thin sticks. The sticks are dyed bright red or lotus pink and dried in the sun. Then they are coated in a fragrant powder. Family recipes use ingredients such as agarwood, cinnamon, star anise, vetiver root and pomelo peel.

Machines now do the heavy splitting and shaping. Mixing, dyeing and fanning out the bundles are still done by hand. The incense is also exported, to India, China, Malaysia and other markets in Asia.

When to go

Weather comes first

The bundles only go out on dry days. On a rainy or very damp day, there may be little to see. Check the forecast the night before, and keep a backup day if you can.

Time of day

Work starts early. Mid-morning is a safe bet, because by then most yards have their bundles out. The midday sun makes the red very strong, but it is harsh on faces. For portraits, look for shade at the edge of a yard.

Season

The weeks before Lunar New Year (Tết) are the busiest, because people buy incense for the holiday. Expect more visitors and photographers at that time too.

How to get there

  • By car: book a car through a ride-hailing app or hire a private driver. The drive takes about an hour each way. Agree on the return trip before you go, or keep the same driver for the day.
  • With a guide: a tour or a photographer can handle the car, the route and talking with the families.

Fees and etiquette

  • Fees: walking the public lanes costs nothing. Some households with large yards or raised platforms ask for a small fee or sell a ticket. Prices vary, so carry small notes and ask before you go in.
  • Ask first: before you enter a yard, and before you photograph someone up close.
  • Hands off the bundles: don't step on, move or sit on them unless the family invites you.
  • Give way: people are carrying, stacking and driving carts. Step aside and keep the path clear.

Photo: HONG SON / Pexels

Pro move: buy a box of incense before you leave. It makes a light souvenir, and it's a simple way to thank the family whose yard you used.

Photo tips

The overhead pattern

The classic shot is the fan of bundles seen from above, like a field of red flowers. You don't need a drone. The village has iron stairs and raised platforms where visitors can shoot from above.

Photo: Quang Nguyen Vinh / Pexels

Portraits among the bundles

Wear a color the yard doesn't have. White, black, soft green and deep blue stand out against the red. Red or pink clothes disappear into the background.

An áo dài and a conical hat is the classic look here.

Lens and phone settings

  • Camera: a wide lens for patterns and whole yards. A 50 mm to 85 mm lens for portraits, so you can stand back and stay out of the way.
  • Phone: the 0.5x lens for overhead shots, 2x or 3x for portraits.

People at work

Hands fanning out a bundle make a strong detail shot. Ask first. If someone says no or keeps working, move on.

FAQ

How far is the incense village from Hanoi?

About 35 km south of central Hanoi, roughly 1 hour each way by car.

Is there an entrance fee?

The lanes are free. Some yards and viewing platforms charge a small fee.

Are the incense sticks out every day?

Only on dry days. When it rains, they stay inside.

What is the best time to visit?

Mid-morning on a sunny day. The weeks before Tết are the busiest.

Can I photograph the workers?

Ask first, and respect a no. People here are at work.

Can I buy incense in the village?

Yes. Incense is sold in the village, including gift boxes.
