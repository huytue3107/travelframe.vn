# Hanoi French Quarter Guide (2026): A Walking Route, Landmarks and Tips

{{IMG:12520304|The Hanoi Opera House with traffic passing in front on a sunny day}}

*By TravelFrame Editorial Team · Updated November 2026*

Hanoi's French Quarter is the calmer neighbor of the Old Quarter. Streets are wider, trees are taller, and many buildings date from the French colonial period, from the late 1800s to the early 1900s.

It sits just south and east of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, so you can walk there from the Old Quarter in 10–15 minutes.

## Quick facts

- **Where:** south and southeast of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, around Tràng Tiền, Ngô Quyền, Lý Thái Tổ, Hai Bà Trưng and Phan Chu Trinh streets.
- **Time needed:** 2–3 hours on foot at an easy pace, longer with a museum.
- **Cost:** walking is free. Museums charge an entry fee.
- **Best time:** early morning or late afternoon, when the light is soft and the streets are cooler.

## A simple walking route

This loop links the best-known buildings. It is about 2–3 km and easy to shorten.

1. **Tràng Tiền street.** Start at the southeast corner of Hoàn Kiếm Lake and walk down Tràng Tiền. Under the French it was rue Paul Bert, the main shopping street linking the lake and the Opera House.
2. **Hanoi Opera House.** Inaugurated in 1911 and modeled on the Palais Garnier in Paris. It still hosts concerts and performances. Read our [guide to Tràng Tiền street and the Opera House](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/trang-tien-street-hanoi-opera-house-locals-guide).
3. **Sofitel Legend Metropole.** On Ngô Quyền street, opened in 1901. It is still a working hotel, so admire the façade from the street.
4. **Government Guest House.** Further along Ngô Quyền, the former residence of the French Governor of Tonkin. It is a state building, so photograph only from the pavement.
5. **State Bank of Vietnam.** On Lý Thái Tổ, the former Bank of Indochina building from the 1930s.
6. **National Museum of Vietnamese History.** Housed in the former building of the École française d'Extrême-Orient, near the Opera House. Worth an hour if you like history.

**Add-ons nearby:**

- [Hỏa Lò Prison](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hoa-lo-prison-witness-history-heart-capital), built by the French, a short walk southwest.
- The [Hanoi Post Office](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hanoi-post-office-three-centuries-flowing-through-name) on the east side of the lake.
- [St. Joseph's Cathedral](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/st-josephs-cathedral) (1886), on the Old Quarter side of the lake.

## Where to pause

- **Ice cream on Tràng Tiền.** The long-running Tràng Tiền ice cream shop is a local habit, especially on warm evenings.
- **Coffee in the side streets.** Small cafés around Lý Thái Tổ and Hai Bà Trưng are quieter than those by the lake.
- **The lakeside.** End your walk on the south shore of Hoàn Kiếm Lake as the light fades.

## Photographing the French Quarter

The quarter rewards slow photography: yellow façades, green shutters, wrought-iron balconies and long tree-lined streets.

- **Morning light** works best on the Opera House square, before traffic builds.
- **Use the trees.** Shoot along the boulevards to frame buildings with leaves and shade.
- **Respect state buildings.** Do not photograph security staff, and follow any "no photo" signs.

## Old Quarter or French Quarter?

- **Old Quarter:** narrow streets, street food, crowds and energy. Best if you want to be in the middle of things.
- **French Quarter:** wider pavements, larger hotels and calmer nights. Better for light sleepers.

Many visitors stay in one and walk to the other. See our [Hanoi Old Quarter guide](https://travelframe.sidevn.com/news/hanoi-old-quarter-guide) for the other half of the picture.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the French Quarter walkable?
Yes. The main sights are within 2–3 km of each other, and pavements are wider than in the Old Quarter.

### How far is the French Quarter from the Old Quarter?
They are separated by Hoàn Kiếm Lake. Walking from the Old Quarter to the Opera House takes about 10–15 minutes.

### Can you go inside the Hanoi Opera House?
The building hosts concerts and performances, so the usual way in is with a ticket to a show. Check the current schedule before you go.

### What is the best time to visit?
Early morning or late afternoon. Midday is hot for most of the year, and the light is flat.
