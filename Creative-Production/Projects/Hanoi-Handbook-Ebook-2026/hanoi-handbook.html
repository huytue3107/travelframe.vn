Welcome

Xin chào, and welcome to Hanoi.

Hanoi rewards people who slow down. It has been Vietnam's capital, on and off, for more than a thousand years, and you can still see the layers: temple roofs between narrow shophouses, French villas under old trees, and street kitchens that have cooked one dish for decades.

We are TravelFrame, a small team of Hanoi locals. We walk the city with travelers and photograph the trip as we go. This handbook is what we tell our own friends before their first visit, in the order you will need it.

01Hanoi at a glance

The basics on one page

Hanoi sits on the Red River in northern Vietnam. The centre is compact: the Old Quarter north of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, the French Quarter to its south, Ba Đình's monuments to the west and West Lake to the north. You can walk between the first three.

Money
Vietnamese đồng (VND)

1 USD ≈ 25,600 VND. Quick maths: 100,000 VND is about US$4. checked Nov 2026

Time
UTC+7, all year

No daylight saving. 12 hours ahead of New York in winter, 11 in summer.

Power
220 V · 50 Hz

Two-pin sockets that take flat (Type A) and round (Type C) plugs.

Language
Vietnamese

English is common in Old Quarter hotels and cafés, less so elsewhere. A translation app helps.

Getting around
Walk + ride apps

Walk the centre. Use Grab, Xanh SM or Be for cars and motorbike taxis.

Water
Bottled or filtered

Don't drink tap water. Ice in cafés and restaurants is usually factory-made.

Tipping
Not expected

Appreciated for guides, drivers and great service. Rounding up is fine.

Entry
Check before you book

Many nationalities need an e-visa, applied for online. See page 4.

Best months
Oct–Nov · Mar–Apr

Mild, mostly dry walking weather. See page 5 for every season.

Emergency
113 · 114 · 115

Police 113 · Fire 114 · Ambulance 115. Save your embassy's number too.

Local rhythmHanoi is a morning city. The lake is busiest at sunrise, many breakfast stalls sell out by 10am, and the streets go quiet after lunch. Plan walks for early and late, and save midday for museums, cafés and a nap.

02Before you fly

Sort these before you leave home

1. Check your entry rule

  • E-visa: many nationalities, including US citizens, need one. Apply only on the official portal evisa.gov.vn. It allows stays of up to 90 days, single or multiple entry. Government fee: US$25 single, US$50 multiple. checked Nov 2026
  • Visa-free: some passports can enter without a visa for a set number of days. The list changes, so check the official immigration portal or a Vietnamese embassy.
  • Passport: valid for at least 6 months after you arrive.
  • Timing: processing is usually about 3 working days. Apply at least a week ahead.
Watch outLookalike visa websites charge extra service fees. The official site ends in .gov.vn.

2. Phone and data

Buy an eSIM before you fly, or a local SIM in the arrivals hall. Local SIMs must be registered with your passport. Main networks: Viettel, Vinaphone, MobiFone.

3. Apps to install

  • Grab, Xanh SM or Be: rides and food delivery
  • Google Maps: download Hanoi for offline use
  • Google Translate: download Vietnamese; the camera reads menus
  • Zalo: many hotels and hosts message on it

4. Money and cover

Bring a card with low foreign fees, a backup card and some clean US dollar or euro notes. Get travel insurance that includes medical care.

Local tipScreenshot your hotel's address in Vietnamese before you land. Every driver can read it.

03When to go

Hanoi has four real seasons

Unlike southern Vietnam, Hanoi gets a proper winter and a hot, stormy summer. For walking and photos, locals pick October–November and March–April.

Feb – Apr

Spring

about 17–25°C

Mild and often grey, with fine drizzle. Tết and peach blossom. Pavements get slippery.

May – Aug

Summer

32–35°C+, humid

Hot, with sudden heavy storms; July and August are the wettest. Lotus season on the lakes.

Sep – Nov

Autumn

about 20–30°C

Locals' favourite. Drier air and golden light. Daisy carts appear in November.

Dec – Jan

Winter

about 13–20°C

Cool and damp. Cold snaps can near 10°C, and most rooms have no heating.

Temperatures are typical ranges from long-term averages. Check a forecast the week you fly.

Tết 2027Lunar New Year falls on Saturday 6 February 2027. The week before is beautiful: flower markets, and kumquat trees riding through town on motorbikes. From New Year's Eve to about the third day, many family shops and street stalls close, transport sells out and some prices rise. Book ahead and plan your meals. Official 2027 holiday dates were not final when we checked. checked Nov 2026
Air qualityWinter and early spring can bring hazy days with poor air. Check an air-quality app each morning. On bad days, a good mask helps and indoor plans win.

04How long and where to stay

Give Hanoi three days if you can

Two full days cover the classics. Three or four let you add a day trip to Ninh Bình or Hạ Long Bay and still have slow mornings. Heading to Hạ Long, Sapa or Hà Giang next? Keep a buffer night in Hanoi.

Old Quarter (Hoàn Kiếm)

Best for: first-timers, food and walking everywhere.

Good to know: lively until late. Ask for a room away from the street.

French Quarter

Best for: wide leafy streets, larger hotels and the Opera House.

Good to know: quieter at night, and about 15 minutes' walk to the Old Quarter.

Ba Đình and Trúc Bạch

Best for: the mausoleum, the citadel and the Temple of Literature, plus a local neighbourhood feel.

Good to know: fewer late-night options.

Tây Hồ (West Lake)

Best for: longer stays, cafés, lakeside cycling and a calmer pace.

Good to know: about 15–25 minutes by car to the Old Quarter.

Local tipCheck the map before you book. "Old Quarter" in a hotel's name doesn't always mean it is inside the Old Quarter.

05Airport to the city

Nội Bài to the Old Quarter

Nội Bài International Airport (HAN) is about 30 km north of the Old Quarter. By car it takes 45–60 minutes, and longer at rush hour or in heavy rain.

Ride-hailing

Connect to airport Wi-Fi or your eSIM, book in the app and follow its pickup signs. Check the plate number before you get in. A small airport toll may be added.

Airport taxi

Use the official taxi queue outside arrivals. Ignore anyone offering a ride inside the terminal. Make sure the meter is running.

Bus 86

Stops outside arrivals at both terminals and runs past the Old Quarter and Hoàn Kiếm Lake to Hanoi Railway Station. Pay on board with small notes. The last bus leaves the airport around 10pm.

Arriving late?

After the last bus, use an app, the taxi queue or a pre-booked pickup. Message your hotel if your flight is delayed.

06Money and prices

Cash for the street, cards for the rest

Cash or card?

Cards work in hotels, many cafés, restaurants, malls and convenience stores. Street food, markets and small shops want cash. Keep 10,000–50,000 VND notes handy.

ATMs and exchange

  • ATMs are everywhere. Each bank sets its own fee and withdrawal limit, and your home bank may add more.
  • Use ATMs inside banks or malls.
  • If a machine offers to charge you in your home currency, choose VND.
  • Exchange cash at banks or authorised exchange counters. Bring clean, undamaged notes.

Notes you'll use

Plastic (polymer) notes: 10,000 · 20,000 · 50,000 · 100,000 · 200,000 · 500,000 VND. Older paper notes of 1,000, 2,000 and 5,000 VND still turn up as change.

Two blue notesThe 20,000 and 500,000 VND notes are both blue. Count them twice before you hand them over.

07Getting around

Walk first, then ride

Crossing the street

  • Walk at a slow, steady pace. Don't run, stop suddenly or step back.
  • Look at oncoming riders. A raised hand helps them see you.
  • Use lights and zebra crossings where they exist, and watch for turning bikes.
  • On big roads, cross next to a local.

More: how to cross the street in Hanoi

Ride-hailing apps

Grab, Xanh SM (all-electric) and Be offer cars and motorbike taxis with the price shown first. Match the plate before you get on, and wear the helmet you're given.

Taxis

Book through an app or use metered taxis from well-known companies. Check that the meter starts.

Cyclo

A slow pedal ride with you in the front seat. Agree the price and the duration before you sit down. Booking through your hotel is easiest.

Metro and buses

Line 2A (Cát Linh–Hà Đông) and the elevated part of Line 3 (Nhổn–Cầu Giấy) run today. The underground section to Hanoi Station is expected in 2027, so the metro doesn't reach the Old Quarter yet. City buses are cheap and air-conditioned, and Google Maps shows the routes. checked Nov 2026

Renting a motorbike?

You need a licence that is valid in Vietnam, or your insurance may not pay out after an accident. In central Hanoi, a motorbike taxi is the easier choice.

Local tipPavements often double as motorbike parking. Walk with the flow and keep your phone on the side away from the road.

08Things to see

Start here: the lake and the Old Quarter

Hoàn Kiếm Lake

The city's living room. Come at sunrise to see tai chi, dancing and joggers before the traffic builds. The red Thê Húc bridge leads to Ngọc Sơn Temple, which has a small entry fee.

On Friday, Saturday and Sunday evenings, the streets around the lake close to traffic from about 7pm to midnight. Families, buskers and street games take over. checked Nov 2026

Free mapOur 2-hour Old Quarter walking route (1.9 km, A4 PDF) is free on the Old Quarter guide.

The Old Quarter

Many street names begin with Hàng, meaning "goods". Each street once sold one thing: Hàng Bạc for silver, Hàng Mã for paper goods, Hàng Đường for sugar. Some still do.

  • Đồng Xuân Market: the biggest covered market in the quarter.
  • 87 Mã Mây: a restored merchant's house, about 20,000 VND.
  • Ô Quan Chưởng: the last old city gate still standing, built in 1749.
  • Bạch Mã Temple: one of the quarter's oldest temples. It closes at midday.

Go early, look up at the balconies, and duck into the narrow lanes.

09Things to see

History and heritage

Temple of Literature

Founded in 1070 to honour Confucius, it became home to Vietnam's first national university in 1076. The 82 stone stelae on turtles list the names of doctoral laureates.

70,000 VND · 8:00–17:00 · cash only. Cover shoulders and knees. In graduation season the courtyards fill with students in gowns. checked Nov 2026

Our Temple of Literature guide

Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long

A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010, with a thousand years of royal layers, excavated foundations and a wartime command bunker. Quiet, shady and often overlooked. Read more

Ba Đình and the Mausoleum

Ho Chi Minh's Mausoleum opens on Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday mornings. It is closed on Mondays and Fridays. Entry is free. checked Nov 2026

  • Shoulders and knees covered, no hats, no talking, no photos inside.
  • Bags and cameras are checked at security.
  • It usually closes for about two months of maintenance in autumn. In 2026 that started on 4 September.

Nearby: the One Pillar Pagoda (first built in 1049) and the Presidential Palace grounds with Ho Chi Minh's stilt house (ticketed).

10Things to see

More to explore

Long Biên Bridge

A steel bridge over the Red River, finished in 1902 and still used by trains, bikes and walkers. Walk the side lane at sunrise and look down at the banana fields on the river bank.

Hỏa Lò Prison

Built by the French in 1896, later known to American POWs as the "Hanoi Hilton". 50,000 VND · 8:00–17:00 daily. The evening "Sacred Night" tour is sold separately and books out early. checked Nov 2026

Museums worth your time

The Vietnamese Women's Museum tells the country's story through family, work and fashion. The Vietnam Museum of Ethnology covers all 54 ethnic groups and has full-size traditional houses in its garden. Check opening days before you go.

Train Street

Trains pass a few steps from people's front doors between Phùng Hưng and Lê Duẩn. Access is controlled and changes without notice. Some days cafés serve visitors; other days barriers are closed.

Safety firstTreat it as an optional stop. Never stand on the tracks, and follow staff instructions straight away. Train Street guide

West Lake and Trấn Quốc Pagoda

Trấn Quốc, with roots in the 6th century, is often called Hanoi's oldest pagoda. Come for sunset over the lake, or cycle the shore on a weekday morning.

The French Quarter

St Joseph's Cathedral (1886), the Opera House (1911) and wide boulevards south of the lake. French Quarter guide

11Plan your days

One, two or three days

Hanoi is best in the morning and late afternoon. These plans keep the middle of the day for museums, cafés and rest. Mix and match.

Day 1 · The lake and the Old Quarter

  1. Sunrise at Hoàn Kiếm Lake, then bánh cuốn for breakfast.
  2. Walk the Old Quarter on our free map: Hàng streets, Đồng Xuân Market, 87 Mã Mây, Ô Quan Chưởng.
  3. Egg coffee on a balcony. Bún chả for lunch, then a rest.
  4. Late afternoon: Ngọc Sơn Temple and a water puppet show.
  5. Evening: the walking street and night market (Fri–Sun), or bia hơi on Tạ Hiện.

Day 2 · History and the French Quarter

  1. Early: Ho Chi Minh's Mausoleum (if it's an opening day), the One Pillar Pagoda and the Presidential Palace grounds.
  2. Temple of Literature before the midday heat.
  3. Lunch, then Hỏa Lò Prison.
  4. Walk the French Quarter: St Joseph's Cathedral, the Opera House, Tràng Tiền ice cream.
  5. Dinner: chả cá, turmeric fish with dill cooked at your table.

Day 3 · Slow Hanoi, or a day trip

  1. Sunrise walk on Long Biên Bridge.
  2. West Lake: Trấn Quốc Pagoda and a lakeside café.
  3. Afternoon: the Museum of Ethnology or the Women's Museum, or the Imperial Citadel.
  4. Sunset by West Lake, then phở cuốn at Trúc Bạch.

Or swap Day 3 for Ninh Bình or Hạ Long Bay (see page 20).

12Eat like a local

Twelve things to eat in Hanoi

Hanoi food is about balance: clear broths, fresh herbs, a squeeze of lime and a little chilli. Many stalls cook only one dish, all day, for years. If you see a crowd of locals on tiny stools, sit down.

Phở bò / phở gà
Beef or chicken noodle soup

The breakfast classic. Add lime, chilli and garlic vinegar to taste.

Bún chả
Grilled pork with noodles

Charcoal-grilled pork in a sweet-sour broth, with rice noodles and herbs. A lunch dish.

Bánh cuốn
Steamed rice rolls

Silky sheets filled with pork and wood-ear mushroom, topped with crispy shallots.

Chả cá
Turmeric fish with dill

Fried at your table with spring onion and dill, eaten with noodles and peanuts.

Bún riêu cua
Crab and tomato noodle soup

Tangy, red and comforting. Often found at street corners in the morning.

Phở cuốn
Fresh phở rolls

Flat rice-noodle sheets rolled around beef and herbs. A Trúc Bạch speciality.

Nem rán
Fried spring rolls

Crisp rolls of pork, glass noodles and mushroom, dipped in fish sauce.

Bún đậu mắm tôm
Tofu with shrimp paste

Fried tofu, noodles and herbs with a bold fermented shrimp dip. Brave and worth it.

Bánh mì
Baguette sandwich

Pâté, cold cuts or egg in a crackly baguette. The perfect quick snack.

Xôi
Sticky rice

A cheap, filling breakfast with toppings like mung bean, fried shallot or chicken.

Cà phê trứng
Egg coffee

Whipped egg yolk and sugar over strong coffee. Invented in Hanoi in the 1940s.

Kem Tràng Tiền
Tràng Tiền ice cream

A Hanoi favourite since the 1950s. Green rice and coconut are good first picks.

Vegetarian?Say Tôi ăn chay (toy an chai), "I eat vegetarian". Look for the word chay. Many vegetarian restaurants sit near pagodas. Our Hanoi Secret Food Map (free PDF) adds where to eat.

13Eat like a local

How to eat street food, and drink coffee

Seven street food habits

  • Follow the locals. Busy stalls with fast turnover are the safest and tastiest.
  • One dish per stall. The sign usually names the dish. That's the menu.
  • Sit low. Tiny plastic stools are normal. Sit down and someone will come to you.
  • Ask the price where nothing is written: Bao nhiêu?
  • Season it yourself. Chilli, lime, garlic and vinegar are on the table.
  • Wet towels in packets may be added to the bill. Leave them sealed if you don't want them.
  • Mind the clock. Many stalls sell only breakfast or only dinner, and close when they sell out.

14Hanoi after dark

Hanoi after dark

Walking street

Fri–Sun, about 7pm to midnight, the streets around Hoàn Kiếm Lake close to traffic. Expect families, music, street games and a lot of people-watching.

Old Quarter night market

Fri–Sun evenings, along Hàng Đào towards Đồng Xuân Market: clothes, souvenirs and snacks. Bargain with a smile. Night market guide

Water puppets

A northern Vietnamese art born in rice paddies. Shows by the lake last about 50 minutes. Buy tickets ahead on the theatre's official website.

Bia hơi and Tạ Hiện

Bia hơi is light draught beer brewed daily and poured by the glass at street corners. Sit on a low stool, order snacks to share and say Một, hai, ba, dzô! (one, two, three, cheers!). Tạ Hiện is the loudest corner. Tạ Hiện guide

Quieter nights

A show at the Opera House, a jazz club, or a rooftop drink looking over the lake.

Local tipHold your phone on the side away from the road when you film the crowds, and keep your bag zipped and in front of you.

Pick your evening

Friday to Sunday

Walking street around the lake, then the night market on Hàng Đào.

Any night

Bia hơi on a corner, a water puppet show, or live music in a jazz bar.

Rainy night

An upstairs café over the lake, egg coffee in hand, watching the lights.

15Respect

Be a good guest

Hanoians are warm and patient with visitors. A few small habits show respect and open a lot of doors, and smiles.

Do

  • Cover shoulders and knees at temples, pagodas and the mausoleum. A light scarf in your bag solves it.
  • Take your shoes off when you see shoes lined up at a door.
  • Ask before you photograph people. Smile, point at your camera and wait for a yes.
  • Use both hands to give or receive something from older people.
  • Greet people with Xin chào and a nod.
  • Bargain calmly and kindly at markets. Fixed-price shops are fixed.

Don't

  • Touch people's heads, including children's.
  • Point your feet at altars or at people.
  • Raise your voice in a disagreement. Calm gets better results.
  • Photograph military sites or police at work.
  • Climb on statues or monuments for a photo.
  • Step past railway barriers or stand on Train Street's tracks.

16Stay safe

Stay safe, skip the traps

Hanoi is a safe, friendly city. Most problems are small, about money, and easy to avoid once you know them.

Phones and bags

Snatch-and-go from passing motorbikes can happen. Keep your phone and bag on the side away from the road, and wear a cross-body bag.

"Free" props and services

A shoe shine you didn't ask for, a fruit basket for a photo. If you don't want it, say Không, cảm ơn before it starts. If you do, agree the full price first.

Prices and bills

Ask before ordering where no price is shown. Check the bill, and count your change, especially the 20,000 and 500,000 notes.

Taxis and cyclos

Use an app or a metered taxi, and ignore touts inside the airport. For cyclos, agree price and duration before you sit down.

Food and water

Choose busy stalls and hot, freshly cooked food. Drink bottled or filtered water. Pharmacies (nhà thuốc) are on almost every street.

Bites, sun and air

Dengue is spread by mosquitoes here, so use repellent, especially in the rainy season. Wear sunscreen in summer, and check air quality in winter.

Ninh Bình in a day

The most popular escape: rowing boats glide between limestone peaks, through caves and past rice fields. Leave early. A full day is tight but doable by car, tour bus or train.

Hạ Long Bay: day or overnight?

A day trip means 5–6 hours on the road for a few hours on the water. An overnight cruise gives you sunset, sunrise and calmer coves.

Tour, driver or train?

A group tour sorts transport and tickets for you. A private car with a driver gives you your own timing and stops. For Ninh Bình, the train is a slower, scenic option. Whichever you choose, leave early to beat the crowds.

19What to pack

Pack light, pack for the season

You can buy almost anything in Hanoi, often for less. Bring the essentials, comfortable shoes and the right layers for the month you travel.

Every season

  • Passport, plus a copy of your e-visa (printed and on your phone)
  • Two bank cards and some cash
  • Phone, charger and power bank
  • Plug adapter if your plugs aren't Type A or C
  • Comfortable walking shoes you have already worn in
  • A light scarf or shawl for temples
  • Small cross-body bag
  • Refillable water bottle
  • Insect repellent and sunscreen
  • Basic medicines and any prescriptions
  • Tissues and hand sanitiser
  • A light rain poncho

Summer · May–Aug

Breathable cotton or linen, a hat, quick-dry layers and sandals that cope with puddles. Electrolyte sachets help on hot days.

Autumn · Sep–Nov

Light clothes for the day and a thin layer for evenings, which cool down from late October.

Winter · Dec–Jan

Real warm layers: a fleece or light down jacket, long trousers and warm socks. Rooms can be cold, as few buildings have heating.

Spring · Feb–Apr

A light waterproof jacket, a mid layer and shoes with grip for damp, slippery pavements.

20Photos and memories

Photos and your Hanoi story

Hanoi is one of Asia's most photogenic cities, if you know when to look.

  • Go early. From 6 to 8am the light is soft and the streets are empty.
  • Look up. Balconies, shutters, plants and the tangle of wires are pure Hanoi.
  • Find the lanes. Narrow alleys (ngõ) hide the quietest frames. An upstairs café gives you the view from above.
  • Love the rain. Wet stone and lights at night make great reflections.
  • Ask first, always. And respect a no.
  • Put the phone down sometimes. Some moments are just for you.
