01Hanoi at a glance

The basics, on one page

Hanoi sits on the Red River in northern Vietnam. The centre is compact: the Old Quarter north of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, the French Quarter to its south, Ba Đình's monuments to the west and West Lake to the north.

Money
Vietnamese đồng (VND)

1 USD ≈ 25,600 VND. Quick maths: 100,000 VND is about US$4. checked Nov 2026

Power
220 V · 50 Hz

Two-pin sockets that take flat (Type A) and round (Type C) plugs.

Getting around
Walk, then ride apps

Walk the centre. Use Grab, Xanh SM or Be for cars and motorbike taxis.

Tipping
Not expected

Appreciated for guides, drivers and great service. Rounding up is fine.

Best months
Oct–Nov · Mar–Apr

Mild, mostly dry walking weather. Every season is on page 6.

Time
UTC+7, all year

No daylight saving. 12 hours ahead of New York in winter, 11 in summer.

Language
Vietnamese

English is common in Old Quarter hotels and cafés, less so elsewhere. A translation app helps.

Water
Bottled or filtered

Don't drink tap water. Ice in cafés and restaurants is usually factory-made.

Entry
Check before you book

Many nationalities need an e-visa, applied for online. See page 5.

Emergency
113 · 114 · 115

Police · Fire · Ambulance. Save your embassy's number too.

Hanoi is a morning city. The lake is busiest at sunrise, breakfast stalls sell out by ten, and the streets go quiet after lunch.
Set an early alarm. It's worth it.

The Hanoi HandbookBefore you fly
02Before you fly

Sort these before you leave home

Four small jobs at home make your first day in Hanoi easy.

1

Check your entry rule

  • E-visa: many nationalities, including US citizens, need one. Apply only on the official portal evisa.gov.vn. It allows stays of up to 90 days, single or multiple entry. Government fee: US$25 single, US$50 multiple.
  • Visa-free: some passports can enter without a visa for a set number of days. The list changes, so check the official immigration portal or a Vietnamese embassy.
  • Passport: valid for at least 6 months after you arrive.
  • Timing: processing is usually about 3 working days. Apply at least a week ahead.
2

Phone and data

Buy an eSIM before you fly, or a local SIM in the arrivals hall. Local SIMs must be registered with your passport. Main networks: Viettel, Vinaphone, MobiFone.

3

Apps to install

  • Grab, Xanh SM or Be for rides and food delivery
  • Google Maps, with Hanoi downloaded for offline use
  • Google Translate, with Vietnamese downloaded; the camera reads menus
  • Zalo, which many hotels and hosts use for messages
4

Money and cover

Bring a card with low foreign fees, a backup card and some clean US dollar or euro notes. Get travel insurance that includes medical care.

Checked

E-visa length and fees Nov 2026

Watch out

Lookalike visa websites charge extra service fees. The official site ends in .gov.vn.

Screenshot your hotel's address in Vietnamese before you land. Every driver can read it.

03When to go

Hanoi has four real seasons

Unlike southern Vietnam, Hanoi gets a proper winter and a hot, stormy summer. For walking and photos, locals pick October–November and March–April.

Spring
Feb – Apr
about 17–25°C

Mild and often grey, with fine drizzle. Tết and peach blossom. Pavements get slippery.

Summer
May – Aug
32–35°C+, humid

Hot, with sudden heavy storms; July and August are the wettest. Lotus season on the lakes.

Autumn
Sep – Nov
about 20–30°C

Locals' favourite. Drier air and golden light. Daisy carts appear in November.

Winter
Dec – Jan
about 13–20°C

Cool and damp. Cold snaps can near 10°C, and most rooms have no heating.

Typical ranges from long-term averages. Check a forecast the week you fly.

Tết 2027 · Saturday 6 February

The week before is beautiful: flower markets, and kumquat trees riding through town on motorbikes. From New Year's Eve to about the third day, many family shops and street stalls close, transport sells out and some prices rise. Book ahead and plan your meals. Official 2027 holiday dates were not final when we checked. Nov 2026

Air quality

Winter and early spring can bring hazy days with poor air. Check an air-quality app each morning. On bad days, a good mask helps and indoor plans win.

The Hanoi HandbookWhere to stay
04How long and where to stay

Give Hanoi three days if you can

Two full days cover the classics. Three or four let you add a day trip to Ninh Bình or Hạ Long Bay and still have slow mornings. Heading to Hạ Long, Sapa or Hà Giang next? Keep a buffer night in Hanoi.

Old Quarter (Hoàn Kiếm)

Best for first-timers, food and walking everywhere. Lively until late, so ask for a room away from the street.

French Quarter

Best for wide leafy streets, larger hotels and the Opera House. Quieter at night, about 15 minutes' walk to the Old Quarter.

Ba Đình and Trúc Bạch

Best for the mausoleum, the citadel and the Temple of Literature, with a local neighbourhood feel. Fewer late-night options.

Tây Hồ (West Lake)

Best for longer stays, cafés, lakeside cycling and a calmer pace. About 15–25 minutes by car to the Old Quarter.

Check the map before you book. "Old Quarter" in a hotel's name doesn't always mean it's in the Old Quarter!

Nội Bài to the Old Quarter

Nội Bài International Airport (HAN) is about 30 km north of the Old Quarter. By car it takes 45–60 minutes, longer at rush hour or in heavy rain.

Ride-hailing

Connect to airport Wi-Fi or your eSIM, book Grab or Xanh SM and follow the app's pickup signs. Check the plate before you get in. A small airport toll may be added.

Airport taxi

Use the official taxi queue outside arrivals. Ignore anyone offering a ride inside the terminal, and make sure the meter is running.

Bus 86

Stops outside arrivals at both terminals and runs past the Old Quarter and Hoàn Kiếm Lake to Hanoi Railway Station. Pay on board with small notes. The last bus leaves the airport around 10pm.

Arriving late?

After the last bus, use an app, the taxi queue or a pre-booked pickup. Message your hotel if your flight is delayed.

06Money and prices

Cash for the street, cards for the rest

1 USD ≈ 25,600 VND. The easy maths: drop the last three zeros and divide by 25. So 100,000 VND is about US$4.

Cash or card?

Cards work in hotels, many cafés, restaurants, malls and convenience stores. Street food, markets and small shops want cash. Keep 10,000–50,000 VND notes handy.

ATMs and exchange

  • ATMs are everywhere. Each bank sets its own fee and limit, and your home bank may add more.
  • Use ATMs inside banks or malls.
  • If a machine offers to charge you in your home currency, choose VND.
  • Exchange cash at banks or authorised exchange counters, with clean, undamaged notes.

The Hanoi HandbookGetting around
07Getting around

Walk first, then ride

Crossing the street, in four rules

1

Walk at a slow, steady pace. Don't run, stop suddenly or step back.

2

Look at oncoming riders. A raised hand helps them see you.

3

Use lights and zebra crossings where they exist. Watch for turning bikes.

4

On big roads, cross next to a local. More: our crossing guide

Ride-hailing apps

Grab, Xanh SM (all-electric) and Be offer cars and motorbike taxis with the price shown first. Match the plate before you get on, and wear the helmet you're given.

Taxis and cyclos

Book a taxi through an app or use metered taxis from well-known companies. A cyclo is a slow pedal ride with you in the front seat: agree the price and duration before you sit down.

Metro and buses

Line 2A (Cát Linh–Hà Đông) and the elevated part of Line 3 (Nhổn–Cầu Giấy) run today. The underground section to Hanoi Station is expected in 2027, so the metro doesn't reach the Old Quarter yet. City buses are cheap, and Google Maps shows the routes. checked Nov 2026

Renting a motorbike?

You need a licence that is valid in Vietnam, or your insurance may not pay out after an accident. In central Hanoi, a motorbike taxi is easier.

08Things to see

Start here: the lake and the Old Quarter

Hoàn Kiếm Lake

The city's living room. Come at sunrise for tai chi, dancing and joggers before the traffic builds. The red Thê Húc bridge leads to Ngọc Sơn Temple, which has a small entry fee.

On Friday, Saturday and Sunday evenings, the streets around the lake close to traffic from about 7pm to midnight. checked Nov 2026

Free walking map

Our 2-hour Old Quarter route (1.9 km, A4 PDF) is free on the Old Quarter guide.

The Old Quarter

Many street names begin with Hàng, "goods". Each street once sold one thing: Hàng Bạc silver, Hàng Mã paper goods, Hàng Đường sugar. Some still do.

  • Đồng Xuân Market: the biggest covered market in the quarter.
  • 87 Mã Mây: a restored merchant's house, about 20,000 VND.
  • Ô Quan Chưởng: the last old city gate still standing, built in 1749.
  • Bạch Mã Temple: one of the oldest temples here. It closes at midday.

A thousand years of capital city, from Confucian courtyards to a revolutionary's resting place.

Temple of Literature

Founded in 1070 to honour Confucius, it became home to Vietnam's first national university in 1076. The 82 stone stelae on turtles list the names of doctoral laureates.

Ba Đình and the Mausoleum

Ho Chi Minh's Mausoleum opens on Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday mornings, and is closed on Mondays and Fridays. Entry is free. checked Nov 2026

  • Shoulders and knees covered, no hats, no talking, no photos inside.
  • Bags and cameras are checked at security.
  • It usually closes for about two months of maintenance in autumn. In 2026 that started on 4 September.

Around Ba Đình

A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010, with royal foundations, excavated layers and a wartime command bunker. Quiet, shady and often overlooked. Read more

More to explore

Long Biên Bridge

A steel bridge over the Red River, finished in 1902 and still used by trains, bikes and walkers. Walk the side lane at sunrise and look down at the banana fields.

Hỏa Lò Prison

Built by the French in 1896, later the "Hanoi Hilton" to American POWs. 50,000 VND, 8:00–17:00 daily. The evening tour is sold separately and books out early. Nov 2026

Two great museums

The Vietnamese Women's Museum tells the country's story through family, work and fashion. The Museum of Ethnology covers all 54 ethnic groups. Check opening days.

Train Street

Trains pass a few steps from front doors between Phùng Hưng and Lê Duẩn. Access is controlled and changes without notice. Treat it as optional, never stand on the tracks, and follow staff at once. Guide

West Lake and Trấn Quốc

Trấn Quốc, with roots in the 6th century, is often called Hanoi's oldest pagoda. Come for sunset over the lake, or cycle the shore on a weekday morning.

The French Quarter

St Joseph's Cathedral (1886), the Opera House (1911) and wide, tree-lined boulevards south of the lake. French Quarter guide

One, two or three days

Hanoi is best in the morning and late afternoon. These plans keep the middle of the day for museums, cafés and rest.

The lake and the Old Quarter

Twelve things to eat in Hanoi

Clear broths, fresh herbs, a squeeze of lime and a little chilli. If you see locals on tiny stools, sit down.

01
Phở bò, phở gà
beef or chicken noodle soup

The breakfast classic. Add lime, chilli and garlic vinegar to taste.

02
Bún chả
grilled pork with noodles

Charcoal-grilled pork in a sweet-sour broth, with rice noodles and herbs. A lunch dish.

03
Bánh cuốn
steamed rice rolls

Silky sheets filled with pork and wood-ear mushroom, topped with crispy shallots.

04
Chả cá
turmeric fish with dill

Fried at your table with spring onion and dill, eaten with noodles and peanuts.

05
Bún riêu cua
crab and tomato noodle soup

Tangy, red and comforting. Often found at street corners in the morning.

06
Phở cuốn
fresh phở rolls

Flat rice-noodle sheets rolled around beef and herbs. A Trúc Bạch speciality.

07
Nem rán
fried spring rolls

Crisp rolls of pork, glass noodles and mushroom, dipped in fish sauce.

08
Bún đậu mắm tôm
tofu with shrimp paste

Fried tofu, noodles and herbs with a bold fermented shrimp dip. Brave, and worth it.

09
Bánh mì
baguette sandwich

Pâté, cold cuts or egg in a crackly baguette. The perfect quick snack.

10
Xôi
sticky rice

A cheap, filling breakfast with toppings like mung bean, fried shallot or chicken.

11
Cà phê trứng
egg coffee

Whipped egg yolk and sugar over strong coffee. Invented in Hanoi in the 1940s.

12
Kem Tràng Tiền
Tràng Tiền ice cream

A Hanoi favourite since the 1950s. Green rice or coconut are good first picks.

Vegetarian?

Say Tôi ăn chay (toy an chai), "I eat vegetarian", and look for the word chay. Many vegetarian restaurants sit near pagodas. Where to eat: our free Hanoi Secret Food Map.

13Eat like a local

Street food and coffee

Seven street food habits

1

Follow the locals. Busy stalls with fast turnover are the safest and tastiest.

2

One dish per stall. The sign usually names the dish. That's the menu.

3

Sit low. Tiny plastic stools are normal. Sit down and someone will come.

4

Ask the price where nothing is written: Bao nhiêu?

5

Season it yourself. Chilli, lime, garlic and vinegar are on the table.

6

Wet towels in packets may be added to the bill. Leave them sealed if you don't want them.

7

Mind the clock. Many stalls sell only breakfast or only dinner, and close when they sell out.

14Hanoi after dark

Hanoi after dark

Walking street

Friday to Sunday, about 7pm to midnight, the streets around Hoàn Kiếm Lake close to traffic. Expect families, music, street games and a lot of people-watching.

Old Quarter night market

Friday to Sunday evenings, along Hàng Đào towards Đồng Xuân Market: clothes, souvenirs and snacks. Bargain with a smile. Night market guide

Water puppets

A northern art born in the rice paddies. Shows by the lake last about 50 minutes. Buy tickets ahead on the theatre's official website.

Bia hơi and Tạ Hiện

Light draught beer brewed daily and poured by the glass at street corners. Sit on a low stool, share some snacks and say Một, hai, ba, dzô! (one, two, three, cheers!). Tạ Hiện is the loudest corner. Tạ Hiện guide

Quieter nights

A show at the Opera House, a jazz club, or an upstairs café over the lake on a rainy evening.

Filming the crowds? Hold your phone on the side away from the road.
Friday to Sunday

Walking street around the lake, then the night market on Hàng Đào.

Any night

Bia hơi on a corner, a water puppet show, or live music in a jazz bar.

Rainy night

An upstairs café over the lake, egg coffee in hand, watching the lights.

15Respect

Be a good guest

Hanoians are warm and patient with visitors. A few small habits show respect, and open a lot of doors.

Do

  • Cover shoulders and knees at temples, pagodas and the mausoleum. A light scarf in your bag solves it.
  • Take your shoes off when you see shoes lined up at a door.
  • Ask before you photograph people. Smile, point at your camera and wait for a yes.
  • Use both hands to give or receive something from older people.
  • Greet people with Xin chào and a nod. Bargain calmly and kindly.

Don't

  • Touch people's heads, including children's.
  • Point your feet at altars or at people.
  • Raise your voice in a disagreement. Calm gets better results.
  • Photograph military sites or police at work.
  • Climb on statues, or step past railway barriers on Train Street.

16Stay safe

Stay safe, skip the traps

Hanoi is a safe, friendly city. Most problems are small, about money, and easy to avoid once you know them.

Phones and bags

Snatch-and-go from passing motorbikes can happen. Keep phone and bag on the side away from the road.

"Free" props

A shoe shine you didn't ask for, a fruit basket for a photo. Say Không, cảm ơn before it starts, or agree the full price first.

Prices and bills

Ask before ordering where no price is shown. Check the bill and count your change.

Taxis and cyclos

Use an app or a metered taxi, and ignore touts in the airport. Agree cyclo prices first.

Food and water

Busy stalls, hot food, bottled water. Pharmacies (nhà thuốc) are on almost every street.

Bites, sun and air

Dengue is spread by mosquitoes, so use repellent. Sunscreen in summer, air checks in winter.

Police
113
Fire
114
Ambulance
115

If something goes wrong, stay calm and ask your hotel to help. Staff can call in Vietnamese and talk to the police for you. For anything serious, contact your embassy and insurer.

19What to pack

Pack light, pack for the season

You can buy almost anything in Hanoi, often for less. Bring the essentials, comfortable shoes and the right layers.

Every season

  • Passport and a copy of your e-visa
  • Two bank cards and some cash
  • Phone, charger and power bank
  • Plug adapter, if yours aren't Type A or C
  • Walking shoes you've already worn in
  • A light scarf for temples
  • Small cross-body bag
  • Refillable water bottle
  • Insect repellent and sunscreen
  • Basic medicines and prescriptions
  • A light rain poncho
Summer May–Aug

Breathable cotton or linen, a hat, quick-dry layers, sandals that cope with puddles, and electrolyte sachets.

Autumn Sep–Nov

Light clothes for the day and a thin layer for evenings, which cool down from late October.

Winter Dec–Jan

Real warm layers: a fleece or light down jacket, long trousers and warm socks. Few rooms have heating.

Spring Feb–Apr

A light waterproof jacket, a mid layer and shoes with grip for damp pavements.

20Photos and memories

Photos and your Hanoi story

Hanoi is one of Asia's most photogenic cities, if you know when to look.

1

Go early. From 6 to 8am the light is soft and the streets are empty.

2

Look up. Balconies, shutters, plants and the tangle of wires are pure Hanoi.

3

Find the lanes. Narrow alleys (ngõ) hide the quietest frames. An upstairs café gives you the view from above.

4

Love the rain. Wet stone and lights at night make great reflections.

5

Ask first, always, and respect a no.
